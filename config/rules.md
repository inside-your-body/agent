# Inside Your Body — content rules (read by the script-writing agent)

## Channel
English health-curiosity channel. Animated 2D cartoon explainers: recurring cartoon characters, 2D illustrations,
kinetic typography and motion graphics, AI narrator.
Audience: curious adults worldwide (US/UK first). Tone: warm, energetic, wonder-filled storytelling.

## What we make (and what we DON'T)
- We EXPLAIN WHAT HAPPENS inside the body — fascinating, step by step, like a documentary adventure.
  "What happens inside your body when you drink alcohol", "...when you don't sleep for 3 days", "...if you drink milk every day",
  "the journey of a cold pill through your body", "how a wound heals, minute by minute", "what happens when the heart stops".
- We do NOT lecture, moralise or tell people what to do. Never "don't smoke", "stop drinking", "you should quit".
  No guilt, no fear-mongering. Facts and the body's reactions speak for themselves.
- The ending is a satisfying conclusion: the outcome of the journey, the most amazing takeaway, what the body is capable of.
  Neutral facts like "here's how the body recovers / what helps it" are fine when they are part of the story, never as orders.
- Topics must be things people actually search for, with a curiosity gap — never trivial or obvious.

## Story format (retention first) — narration 1500–1600 words (≈ 8:30)
The whole video is ONE story with characters, told by the narrator.
1. HOOK (0–15 s): a striking moment or question ("In the next 8 minutes, a tiny pill is going to fight a war inside you.").
   No channel intro before it.
2. THE SITUATION (problem/trigger): our human character (named, relatable) gets into the situation — catches a cold,
   gets an IV drip at the clinic, cuts a finger, stays awake all night, drinks the first beer...
3. THE JOURNEY: follow it inside the body step by step on a clock/timeline (minute 1, minute 20, hour 6, day 3...).
   Body parts and agents are CARTOON CHARACTERS with personalities: the pill/drip/antibiotic as a hero, white blood cells as
   guards/soldiers, viruses and bacteria as villains, the liver as an overworked factory worker, the brain as the control room...
4. CLIMAX: the biggest twist / the battle / the surprising moment.
5. RESOLUTION: what finally happens + the "wow" takeaway, then one question for the comments and subscribe.
- 3–4 open loops / mid-video hooks ("but at hour six, something strange happens…").
- Change the visual every 6–12 seconds. Sentences short and spoken, second person "you" mixed with the character's story.

## Accuracy & safety
- Every number or claim must come from a reputable source (WHO, CDC, NHS, NIH/PubMed, Mayo Clinic, major journals). List sources.
- Cartoon metaphors are fine, but the biology behind them must be correct.
- No diagnosis or individual medical advice; no miracle cures, fake statistics or guaranteed outcomes.

## Short (one per long video) — 40–48 s, vertical
- Hook text + voice from frame 1 (no logo). The single most fascinating moment of the episode, told as a mini story.
- Fast visual change every 1–3 s, big word-by-word captions in the centre safe area.
- One "send this to someone who…" line. Last sentence flows back into the first so the Short loops.
- Its own visual style, different from the long video.

## Visuals
- Every episode gets a NEW style (palette, fonts, motion language, transitions, music mood) — never the same as the last 5.
- The core visual is 2D CARTOON: full cartoon scenes with the recurring cast (kind "cartoon") and character cut-outs that
  walk in, bounce, fight (kind "illustration" with "cast"). Mix with kinetic type, body maps, counters, timelines, charts,
  stick figures, split screens where they explain better.
- Style references come from the Pinterest reference board; recreate ideas originally, never copy a pin.
- Keep text inside safe areas; never overlap captions with graphics. Never show gore.

## Thumbnail
- One clear, emotional cartoon image (our character's shocked face, the pill hero facing a virus army, a glowing organ...),
  huge contrast, readable on a phone.
- big_text: 2–4 punchy curiosity words that do NOT repeat the title ("YOUR LIVER PANICS", "HOUR 6 IS WILD", "NOBODY TELLS YOU").
  small_text: optional 2–4 words.

## Upload package
Title ≤ 70 chars, curiosity + search keyword ("What Happens Inside Your Body When…"). Every title different in wording.
Description: 2-line hook, what you'll see, chapters, sources, 3–5 hashtags. 15–20 tags. Pinned comment with a question.
Short: title ≤ 60 chars + #Shorts, link to the full episode.
