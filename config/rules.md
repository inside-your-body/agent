# Inside Your Body — content rules (read by the script-writing agent)

## Channel
English health-curiosity channel. Animated 2D cartoon explainers: recurring cartoon characters, 2D illustrations,
kinetic typography and motion graphics, AI narrator.
Audience: curious adults worldwide (US/UK first). Tone: warm, energetic, wonder-filled storytelling.

## What we make (and what we DON'T)
- We EXPLAIN WHAT HAPPENS inside the body — fascinating, step by step, like a documentary adventure.
  "What happens inside your body when you drink alcohol", "...when you don't sleep for 3 days", "...if you drink milk every day",
  "the journey of a cold pill through your body", "how a wound heals, minute by minute", "what happens when the heart stops".
- We do NOT lecture, moralise or tell people what to do. Never "don't smoke", "stop drinking", "you should quit".
  No guilt, no fear-mongering. Facts and the body's reactions speak for themselves.
- The ending is a satisfying conclusion: the outcome of the journey, the most amazing takeaway, what the body is capable of.
  Neutral facts like "here's how the body recovers / what helps it" are fine when they are part of the story, never as orders.
- Topics must be things people actually search for, with a curiosity gap — never trivial or obvious.

## Story format (retention first) — narration 1200–1320 words (≈ 8:30–9:15)
The whole video is ONE story with characters, told by the narrator.
1. HOOK (0–30 s, where we lose most viewers): cold open in the most surprising visual moment, a burning question, a concrete payoff promise by ~20 s, no background before 0:30. A striking moment or question ("In the next 8 minutes, a tiny pill is going to fight a war inside you.").
   No channel intro before it.
2. THE SITUATION (problem/trigger): our human character (named, relatable) gets into the situation — catches a cold,
   gets an IV drip at the clinic, cuts a finger, stays awake all night, drinks the first beer...
3. THE JOURNEY: follow it inside the body step by step on a clock/timeline (minute 1, minute 20, hour 6, day 3...).
   Body parts and agents are CARTOON CHARACTERS with personalities: the pill/drip/antibiotic as a hero, white blood cells as
   guards/soldiers, viruses and bacteria as villains, the liver as an overworked factory worker, the brain as the control room...
4. CLIMAX: the biggest twist / the battle / the surprising moment.
5. RESOLUTION: what finally happens + the "wow" takeaway, then one question for the comments and subscribe.
- 3–4 open loops / mid-video hooks ("but at hour six, something strange happens…").
- Change the visual every 6–12 seconds. Sentences short and spoken, second person "you" mixed with the character's story.

## Accuracy & safety
- Every number or claim must come from a reputable source (WHO, CDC, NHS, NIH/PubMed, Mayo Clinic, major journals). List sources.
- Cartoon metaphors are fine, but the biology behind them must be correct.
- No diagnosis or individual medical advice; no miracle cures, fake statistics or guaranteed outcomes.

## Short (one per long video) — 40–48 s, vertical
- Hook text + voice from frame 1 (no logo). The single most fascinating moment of the episode, told as a mini story.
- Fast visual change every 1–3 s, big word-by-word captions in the centre safe area.
- One "send this to someone who…" line. Last sentence flows back into the first so the Short loops.
- Its own visual style, different from the long video.

## Visuals
- Every episode gets a NEW style (palette, fonts, motion language, transitions, music mood) — never the same as the last 5.
- MOTION GRAPHICS and KINETIC TYPOGRAPHY are the backbone (most of the video): kinetic type, body maps, counters,
  timelines, charts, split screens, checklists, icons, stick figures explain the science.
- The 2D cartoon cast complements them at key story moments: full cartoon scenes (kind "cartoon") and character
  cut-outs that walk in, bounce, fight (kind "illustration" with "cast").
- Typography, motion and colour ideas come from the saved Pinterest reference pins; recreate ideas originally, never copy a pin.
- Keep text inside safe areas; never overlap captions with graphics. Never show gore.

## Storyboard & motion rules (owner's standing instructions)
- Storyboard first: for every sentence decide what we SEE, then build the shot from it.
- Whenever the narrator speaks, the screen shows what is being talked about (tongue → a tongue; bacteria eating → bacteria eating). Never an empty or unrelated screen.
- NO REPETITION at all: a kind never repeats within 5 shots, every drawn shot is a new drawing (no reused images), text-only screens are never back to back; characters get new poses/expressions/actions as the story changes.
- NO EMPTY SCREEN: whatever the narrator names (tongue, bacteria, heart...) is visible on screen in that moment.
- Shorts: captions live in their own bottom band and never overlap other text or graphics.
- Audio: only commercially licensed sound (CC0 / own work) — see LICENSES.md.

## Thumbnail
- The viewer must understand the TOPIC at a glance: a topic tag with the subject in 1–3 words ("IV DRIP", "BAD BREATH",
  "HEART ATTACK", "NO SLEEP 72H") + a curiosity line of 2–4 words that makes them want the answer
  ("WHAT HAPPENS INSIDE YOU?", "WHERE DOES IT GO?", "HOUR 6 IS WILD").
- One clear, emotional cartoon image (shocked face, the hero facing the villain, a glowing organ), huge contrast, phone-readable.
- thumbnail.big_text = the topic tag; thumbnail.small_text = the curiosity line.

## Topics
- Model topics and title patterns on health videos that are PROVEN to get millions of views (the weekly scout finds them):
  make our own version of the same idea — never copy, but don't be afraid to cover what already works.

## Upload package
Title ≤ 70 chars: a plain, clear question or statement that names the topic the way people search it
("Why Does Your Breath Smell Bad?", "What Happens Inside Your Body When You Get an IV Drip?"). Every title different in wording.
Description: 2-line hook, what you'll see, chapters, sources, 3–5 hashtags. 15–20 tags. Pinned comment with a question.
Short: title ≤ 60 chars + #Shorts, link to the full episode.

## Lessons from our analytics (Oct 2026)
- Biggest drop is the first minute → the opening must be a cold open + payoff promise, all visual.
- Thumbnail CTR was 1.3–2% → 3 thumbnail concepts per episode (Test & compare); topic must be readable in 1 second.
- Search terms that brought viewers were concrete ("tonsil stones", "first cigarette") → titles/tags use exact search phrases.
- Shorts bring ~97% of views but almost nobody goes to the long video → every Short points to the full episode (line + comment + link).
- 10:50 was too long for current retention → 8:30–9:15.

## Motion design (owner's standing instruction, Oct 2026)
- Every key moment gets a BRAND-NEW animation designed and coded from scratch for that moment (kind "gen") — no templates.
  Each is QA-checked (stills + art review) and redesigned once if it fails; failures become a new drawing, never a template.
- Every video has its own DESIGN LANGUAGE (art style, shape language, line, texture, motion personality, camera, typography),
  clearly different from recent videos — drawings, animations and the Short all follow it.
