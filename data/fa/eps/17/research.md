## Seconds to hours (every session)

1. **Brain effects begin within seconds.** Smoked THC reaches the blood quickly, with psychotropic effects starting within seconds to a few minutes, peaking at 15–30 minutes, and tapering off within 2–3 hours. Source: Clinical Pharmacokinetics of Cannabinoids (review). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228425785_Clinical_Pharmacokinetics_of_Cannabinoids

2. **Blood THC peaks and then drops fast.** Smoked and vaporized cannabis cause rapid rises of THC and 11-OH-THC in blood, peaking within 10–30 minutes and returning to baseline within 1–4 hours. Source: PMC (acute pharmacokinetics study). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6676961/

3. **Surprise: you're still high after the blood level has crashed.** Plasma THC is only 15–20% of peak at 30 minutes, 8–10% at 60 minutes, and 2–3% at 180 minutes. The high lags behind the blood level: peak effects occur after blood concentration has peaked and begun to decline. That is why blood tests don't show how impaired someone is. Sources: bioRxiv preprint (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.07.02.450963.full.pdf); patent text citing PK tables, so treat it as secondary (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10969333).

4. **Heart rate jumps within minutes.** Within minutes, heart rate may rise by 20 to 50 beats per minute, and the rapid rate can continue for up to 3 hours. A Harvard researcher put it at about 40 beats a minute. Sources: Healthline (https://www.healthline.com/health/effects-of-cannabis-on-body); Harvard Gazette (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2000/03/marijuana-said-to-trigger-heart-attacks/)

5. **The "stand up and see stars" moment.** Blood pressure increases, then abruptly falls when the person stands up. The Ottawa Heart Institute notes that blood pressure can drop shortly after use, causing dizziness or light-headedness. Sources: Harvard Gazette (link above); https://www.ottawaheart.ca/patients-visitors/tools-and-resources/cannabis-and-your-heart-health

6. **Surprise: the first-hour heart attack spike.** In a study of 3,882 heart attack survivors, relative risk jumped 4.8 times in the first hour after smoking, then dropped to 1.7 times in the second hour. The absolute risk is small: about 1 in 100,000 for a healthy 50-year-old without other risk factors. Source: Harvard Gazette / Mittleman, Circulation (link above). A 2025 ACC release adds that cannabis users under 50 were over six times as likely to have a heart attack, though that was a retrospective study. https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2025/03/17/15/35/cannabis-users-face-substantially-higher-risk

7. **Munchies are a brain hijack, not a stomach thing.** THC stimulates cannabinoid receptors in the hypothalamus, overriding natural satiety signals to trigger intense, brain-mediated hunger. Researchers say it enhances the motivational value of food, tricking the brain into thinking it is in an acute fasting state. Sources: Neuroscience News (https://neurosciencenews.com/cannabis-munchies-brain-mechanism-30155/); Live Science (https://www.livescience.com/health/why-does-cannabis-give-people-the-munchies)

8. **Why you forget what you were saying.** CB1 receptors are abundant in the hippocampus, the brain region most associated with memory. Its effects resemble a temporary hippocampal lesion. This source is a popular-science article from 2011, so check it against the Han et al., Cell 2012 paper, which links the impairment to astroglial CB1 receptors. https://gizmodo.com/what-cannabis-actually-does-to-your-brain-5794209

## Days to weeks (the "one month" part)

9. **Lungs: the damage is visible early.** Bronchoscopy studies show bronchial inflammation at much lower levels of cannabis use than in tobacco smokers, and symptoms often occur in relatively light users (once a week or more). The American Lung Association says smoke injures the cell linings of the large airways. Sources: Respirology 2024 (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/resp.14676); https://www.lung.org/quit-smoking/smoking-facts/health-effects/marijuana-and-lung-health

10. **Myth check: lung cancer and "it's natural."** Cannabis smoke contains toxic chemicals including ammonia and hydrogen cyanide, but NIDA says there is no conclusive evidence it causes lung cancer. NASEM found substantial evidence linking long-term smoking to worse respiratory symptoms and more frequent bronchitis. Sources: Healthline (link above); https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24625/chapter/9

11. **Tolerance starts within days.** The brain responds to constant THC by turning down its CB1 receptors. A secondary source says tolerance develops within days via CB1 downregulation. A hard NIH-linked number is that chronic heavy users show about 20% lower CB1 availability than controls on PET. Sources: https://www.marijuanaresearch.com/ask/cannabis-tolerance (secondary); https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/63/NCT05664763/Prot_SAP_000.pdf (NIH-funded protocol citing Hirvonen et al.). Note that the ~20% figure comes from chronic daily smokers of years, not from one month of use.

12. **Sleep: a trade-off you can use as a hook.** Acutely, THC appears to delay and suppress REM sleep. Evidence on sleep architecture overall is mixed, because a polysomnography meta-analysis found no consistent overall changes. Source: Springer sleep-medicine review. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44470-026-00095-7

13. **THC hides in fat for weeks.** THC is lipophilic and easily stored in fat, giving a long elimination half-life. Even the "inactive" metabolite THC-COOH has a half-life of 5.2 to 6.2 days, and up to several days or even weeks in very heavy users. Another source reports 1.3 days in infrequent users and 5–13 days in chronic users. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11-Nor-9-carboxy-THC (secondary, cites primary literature); https://www.seaweeddelivery.com/blogs/news/cannabis-half-life-explained-detection-clearance-times (weak source, verify before use)

14. **Surprise: a drug test can be positive long after the high.** Infrequent users test positive for about one to three days; chronic heavy users can stay positive for ten to thirty days or longer. The American College of Medical Toxicology agrees that in chronic heavy users THC-COOH may remain detectable for a month or longer. Source: American Screening Corp summarizing the NIH overview and the ACMT statement. https://www.americanscreeningcorp.com/blogs/news/how-long-does-thc-stay-in-urine

## After quitting: what the body does in the next month

15. **Withdrawal timeline.** Most symptoms appear within 1 day of abstinence, peak within 2–3 days, and typically resolve within 1–2 weeks. Another source says about 76% of daily users who stop abruptly report sleep disturbance. Source: NIH clinical trial protocol (FAAH inhibitor trial). https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/87/NCT03386487/Prot_SAP_000.pdf

16. **Surprise: the brain starts recovering within days.** A PET study of daily smokers in a closed research unit found CB1 availability about 15% lower than in controls at baseline, with the difference no longer statistically significant after 2 and 28 days of abstinence. The earlier NIMH study's conclusion was that chronic daily smoking is associated with reversible downregulation of cortical CB1 receptors. It scanned 14 men after 26 ± 5 days of monitored abstinence. Sources: https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/52/supplement_1/10; https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/63/NCT05664763/Prot_SAP_000.pdf

17. **Vivid dreams are REM rebound.** After quitting, withdrawal reliably causes longer time to fall asleep, shorter total sleep and REM rebound, persisting through at least two weeks of abstinence. Source: Springer review (link above).
