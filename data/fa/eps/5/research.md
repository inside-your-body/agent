## Timeline: what eggs do inside your body

**Minutes to hours (digestion and absorption)**

1. **Cooked vs. raw protein absorption.** In a human isotope-tracer study, true ileal digestibility was 90.9% for cooked egg protein versus 51.3% for raw. A 25 g dose of raw egg protein leaves almost 50% malabsorbed over 24 hours. The study was small, with 5 healthy humans. Source: Evenepoel et al., 1998, *J Nutrition*. https://fitchef.com/shorts/does-cooking-destroy-protein/

2. **Why raw is worse (myth-buster).** Raw egg white contains avidin, which resists digestion and binds biotin (vitamin B7); heat neutralizes it. Raw white also contains trypsin inhibitors, which block a protein-digesting enzyme. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-much-protein-is-in-an-egg-the-complete-breakdown/ and https://www.3dmusclejourney.com/blog/are-raw-eggs-hurting-your-gains

3. **Rocky myth: raw eggs don't build more muscle.** Despite the absorption gap, post-exercise muscle protein synthesis rates were virtually identical: 0.067%/h raw vs 0.065%/h cooked. Raw eggs also carry a Salmonella risk. https://fitchef.com/shorts/raw-vs-cooked-eggs-muscle/ (secondary source, so verify against the original paper before using it in the video).

4. **Gold-standard protein.** Egg scores 118 on the non-truncated PDCAAS scale, with 98% digestibility. https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-egg-protein-types-benefits-and-absorption/

5. **Intestines absorb the cholesterol.** After eating eggs, the intestines absorb dietary cholesterol. A large yolk has about 275 mg (this source's figure; other sources give ~186–215 mg). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7400894/

**Hours (hormones, appetite, gut microbes)**

6. **Hunger hormones shift after breakfast.** In an egg vs. bagel crossover, glucose and insulin responses were lower and ghrelin, the only known hunger-stimulating hormone, was suppressed. Participants felt fuller and had less desire to eat over the next 24 hours. In another trial, energy intake was reduced at a lunch 4 hours after an egg breakfast. Source: Ratliff et al., 2010, *Nutrition Research*. https://www.egginfo.co.uk/egg-nutrition-and-health/eggs-and-weight-loss/satiety (the site is egg-industry run) and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7432073/

7. **Fullness hormone PYY rises.** An egg breakfast raised PYY, the fullness hormone, and lowered ghrelin (presented at the European Congress on Obesity, 2012). https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wake-up-to-eggs--a-protein-rich-egg-breakfast-can-act-as-natures-appetite-suppressant-151352385.html (press release).

8. **Gut bacteria turn choline into TMAO.** Gut bacteria convert some ingested choline to trimethylamine (TMA), which travels to the liver and is converted to TMAO. Plasma TMAO peaks 6–8 h after ingestion and returns to baseline within 24 hours. About 11–15% of dietary choline from an egg meal can become TMAO. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/7/4/2731 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693793/

9. **Surprising: your microbiome is required.** After a week of broad-spectrum antibiotics, TMAO was completely suppressed. There was also a more than 4-fold difference in TMAO between subjects at similar doses. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/7/4/2731 (the antibiotic finding comes from a patent document, so cross-check it against the original paper). Longer term, 3 eggs/day for 4 weeks did not change TMAO versus baseline in people with metabolic syndrome. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/6/1179

**Days to weeks (nutrients and cholesterol)**

10. **Choline.** One large hard-boiled egg has 147 mg choline, 27% of the Daily Value. The adult Adequate Intake is 550 mg/day for men and 425 mg/day for women. The brain uses choline to build acetylcholine, a messenger supporting memory and cell signaling. Source: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/

11. **Eye pigments build up in blood.** In a daily two-egg trial, plasma lutein rose from 167 to 369 ng/mL in the enriched-egg group. Lutein and zeaxanthin accumulate in the retina and help protect against oxidative damage. They travel mainly on HDL particles. In another trial, plasma lutein and zeaxanthin rose significantly after egg intake versus baseline or a choline supplement. The ALGOVUE trial used lutein-enriched eggs, not ordinary eggs. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8541434/ and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8951625/

12. **Four weeks of eggs vs. oatmeal.** Fasting ghrelin was significantly lower after four weeks of egg breakfasts, which suggests less hunger on waking. LDL and HDL both rose, but the LDL/HDL ratio did not increase. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9020089

13. **Cholesterol: a small rise.** A meta-analysis of randomized trials found eggs raised total cholesterol by 5.60 mg/dL, LDL by 5.55 mg/dL and HDL by 2.13 mg/dL. There was no effect on LDL:HDL ratio or triglycerides. Source: PubMed, Rouhani et al. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29111915/

14. **The myth twist: saturated fat matters more than egg cholesterol.** In a 5-weeks-per-diet crossover, LDL tracked saturated fat intake (p = 0.002), not dietary cholesterol (p = 0.42). Compared with a high-saturated-fat diet, 2 eggs daily within a low-saturated-fat diet lowered LDL. A caveat: the same study saw fewer large LDL particles and more small, more atherogenic ones. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40339906/ and https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/eggs-do-not-raise-blood-cholesterol-results-of-a-randomised-controlled-trial/47EF5DA93D2B84431CC1BC0BBD504DFF

**Months to years (disease risk)**

15. **Heart disease: the evidence is split.** A BMJ 2020 pooled analysis (33 risk estimates, 1.72 million people) found one egg per day was not associated with CVD risk (pooled RR 0.98). A JAMA 2019 pooled cohort found each extra half egg per day was associated with higher CVD risk (HR 1.06) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.08). A *Circulation* meta-analysis found an 8% increased CVD risk per egg per day in US cohorts, and no association in Asian cohorts. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8137614/ is the dose-response analysis. The main sources are https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2020/03/moderate-egg-consumption-gets-the-green-light-again/, https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2728487 and https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.057642

16. **Diabetes: a US-only signal.** A 16-cohort meta-analysis (589,559 people) found a pooled RR of 1.07 per egg/day (95% CI 0.99–1.15), which is not significant. By region, there was an 18% higher risk per daily egg in US studies, neutrality in Europe, and an 18% lower risk in Asia. Researchers suggest the US signal may reflect smoking, low activity or processed meat eaten alongside eggs, such as bacon. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002916522008358 and https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(22)00819-X/fulltext

## Video notes
- **Strongest hook:** the gut-bacteria-to-liver TMAO pathway peaking 6–8 hours later (#8–9). It is the most surprising "what happens inside" moment.
- **Best myth to bust:** "raw eggs are better" (#1–3), and "egg cholesterol = your cholesterol," because saturated fat matters more (#14).
- **Everyday scene:** a fried-egg breakfast, then no mid-morning snack craving (#6–7, #12).
- **Caveats:**
  - "Weeks and months" claims rest on short trials, mostly 4–11 weeks.
  - There are no randomized trials on years of daily eggs, only observational cohorts.
  - Several studies were funded or summarized by egg industry groups.
