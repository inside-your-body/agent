## Timeline facts

**Seconds to minutes**

1. **Eating starts a signal to the colon within minutes.** When your stomach stretches, nerves signal the colon to start moving. When food enters your stomach, nerves send signals to the muscles in your colon that trigger them to start moving. The colon responds with a series of large, wave-like contractions, called "mass movements." One clinical study found that colonic motor activity normally peaks 10 to 20 minutes after eating.
   - Source: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/gastrocolic-reflex
   - Source: https://biologyinsights.com/what-causes-an-overactive-gastrocolic-reflex/ (secondary source citing PubMed)

2. **The gastrocolic reflex moves old food out, not the meal you just ate.** This is the "why do I poop right after eating?" myth-bust. Your gastrocolic reflex tells your bowels to move older food out to make room for the new. The messengers are the hormones cholecystokinin and gastrin.
   - Source: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/gastrocolic-reflex
   - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/veterinary-science-and-veterinary-medicine/gastrocolic-reflex

**Hours 0–5: the stomach**

3. **The stomach empties in stages.** In a 123-volunteer, 11-centre study, the median amount still in the stomach was 69% at one hour, 24% at two hours and 1.2% at four hours. Fat slows it down: a heavy fatty meal can linger five to six hours.
   - Source: https://thenaturallaw.com/blogs/how-long-different-foods-take-to-digest-and-the-effect-on-weight-loss/ (secondary source; trace the primary gastric-emptying paper)
   - Source: https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-does-food-stay-in-your-stomach-a-timed-tour-of-digestion/

4. **The small intestine does the absorbing.** The small intestine absorbs most of the nutrients in your food, and your circulatory system passes them on. The lymph system takes the fats: it absorbs fatty acids and vitamins. Transit takes roughly 2–6 hours, and the tube is about 20 feet long when stretched out.
   - Source (NIDDK): https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works
   - Source (timing, 20-foot length): https://www.gbchealth.org/blog/why-do-i-have-to-poop-right-after-i-eat/

5. **Most of the nutrition is gone before the colon.** By the time material exits the small intestine, your body has already extracted the vast majority of calories, vitamins, and minerals. Water goes too: by the time indigestible materials reach the colon, up to 90% of the water has been absorbed by the small intestine.
   - Source: https://scienceinsights.org/how-long-does-it-take-to-digest-food-and-poop-it-out/
   - Source (StatPearls, NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK507857/

**Day 1–3: the colon**

6. **Most of the journey is spent in the colon.** Typical stage times are two to five hours in the stomach, two to six hours in the small intestine, and roughly 12 to 48 hours in the colon. The total is usually 24–72 hours.
   - Source: https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-does-food-stay-in-your-stomach-a-timed-tour-of-digestion/
   - NIDDK also cites 24–72 hours, via https://nationalreport.uk/blog/digestive-system-guide/

7. **The colon reabsorbs water and electrolytes.** The colon absorbs approximately 400mL of water per day. Another source says it absorbs on average 1–1.5 liters of fluid every day and can adapt to as much as 5 liters per day if needed. The two sources disagree, so quote a range or pick one source.
   - Source: https://teachmephysiology.com/gastrointestinal-system/large-intestine/absorption-large-intestine/
   - Source: https://iffgd.org/gi-disorders/the-digestive-system/

8. **Bacteria make vitamins and short-chain fatty acids.** Bacteria in your large intestine help break down remaining nutrients and make vitamin K. They ferment undigested carbohydrates and fibre into short-chain fatty acids, including acetate, propionate and butyrate. Colon cells use these as fuel.
   - Source (NIDDK): https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works
   - Source: https://teachmephysiology.com/gastrointestinal-system/large-intestine/absorption-large-intestine/

**What poop is made of**

9. **Poop is about 75% water, and much of the rest is bacteria.** Bacterial biomass, both alive and dead, makes up 25–54 percent of the dry weight of poop. The rest is mostly undigested fibre, protein, fat, and dead cells from the gut wall.
   - Source: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324254

10. **There are about 100 billion bacteria per gram of wet stool, and about half are alive.** It's estimated there are nearly 100 billion bacteria per gram of wet stool. A study of fresh stools collected in oxygen-free conditions found almost 50 per cent of the bacteria were alive.
    - Source: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-11-01/poo-bacteria-faeces-microbes/10454142

11. **The brown colour comes from broken-down red blood cells.** A breakdown product of red blood cells and bile, called stercobilin, is responsible for the brown color. On the way through, bacteria transform bilirubin into new compounds that gradually shift from green to yellow to brown.
    - Source: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324254
    - Source: https://biologyinsights.com/what-is-poop-what-its-made-of-and-whats-normal/

**Days to weeks: the gut lining and individual variation**

12. **Your gut lining replaces itself every 3–5 days.** The intestinal epithelium is replenished every 3–4 days by rapidly dividing Lgr5+ intestinal stem cells in the crypt base, until cells eventually slough into the lumen. Those shed cells end up in your poop. Other reviews give 4–5 days, so say "every few days." Paneth cells are an exception: they have an extended lifespan of around 30 days.
    - Source (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7688923/
    - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/intestinal-epithelium

13. **The "Blue Poo" study measured transit time in thousands of people.** The ZOE PREDICT 1 study of 863 adults used blue food dye in muffins as a transit marker and found a median transit time of 28.7 hours, ranging from about 12 hours to several days. A larger citizen-science version with 21,541 volunteers in 17 countries found transit time longest for people living in Spain and shortest in India. Its mean was 23.9 hours, so your video should say 24–29 hours is a typical median in dye studies.
    - Source (secondary summary of Asnicar et al., Gut 2021): https://poopcheck.app/poopcheck-blog/how-long-food-takes-to-digest/
    - Source (Digestive Disease Week 2023 report): https://www.gastroendonews.com/PRN/Article/08-23/gut-transit-time-blue-poop-study/71040

14. **Women's guts run slower than men's.** Healthy women had a longer whole-gut transit than men (median 2.4 and 1.9 days). A wireless-capsule study of 215 adults found women had longer whole-gut transit than men by a median of about four hours. For women, transit times approaching 100 hours can still fall within an acceptable range.
    - Source (PubMed-indexed study via ResearchGate): https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10882189_Gender_Differences_in_Gut_Transit_Shown_with_a_Newly_Developed_Radiological_Procedure
    - Source: https://thenaturallaw.com/blogs/how-long-different-foods-take-to-digest-and-the-effect-on-weight-loss/

15. **Lying in bed slows the colon and exercise speeds it up.** In elderly active men, brief bed rest nearly doubled total colonic transit time, from about 11 hours to roughly 20 hours. In another trial, moderate exercise cut whole-gut transit from about 51 hours to roughly 34 to 37 hours. Both come from a secondary summary of PubMed and Gut papers, so verify the originals.
    - Source: https://biologyinsights.com/how-long-does-poop-stay-in-your-body/

**Myths and everyday situations**

16. **Swallowed gum does not stay for 7 years.** Mayo Clinic says your body can't digest chewing gum, but it doesn't stay in your stomach and passes out in your stool. A Cleveland Clinic dietitian says it takes about 40 hours, just as food does. The rare exception is large amounts of gum combined with constipation, which has blocked intestines in children.
    - Source (Mayo Clinic): https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/indigestion/expert-answers/digestive-system/faq-20058446
    - Source: https://www.livescience.com/health/does-gum-really-take-7-years-to-digest

17. **"Meat rots in your colon for days" is a myth.** Protein is almost completely absorbed in the small intestine, well before the colon. The corn you see in your stool is a related everyday example. Noting when eaten corn reappears gives a rough measure of your gut transit time.
    - Source: https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-digest-food-a-journey-measured-in-hours/

## Gaps and cautions
- **Weeks and months:** I found no strong primary source for these timescales. Facts 12 and 15 are the closest, and you could also frame this as "your gut lining replaces itself about 6–7 times a month."
- **Source quality:** Several transit-time figures come from health blogs summarising Mayo Clinic, Gut, and PubMed studies. For on-screen numbers, cite the original papers, such as Degen & Phillips (Gut 1996) and Asnicar et al. (Gut 2021).
- **Conflicting figures:** Colon transit is variously given as 12–48 hours, 10–59 hours, or about 35–40 hours on average. Total transit averages about 53 hours in the Mayo-style radiopaque-marker studies but about 29 hours in the Blue Poo dye study. The method matters, so it's worth saying so in the video.
