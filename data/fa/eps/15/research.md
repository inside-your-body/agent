## Timeline: what happens inside the body

**Seconds to minutes**
1. **Stomach fills quickly.** After a 240 mL drink, the stomach's liquid volume rose to 242 ± 9 mL after 2 min. (Fasted-state study, Mol Pharm.) https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/mp500210c
2. **Acid dips only briefly.** One 12-person crossover study found that drinking water increased the stomach's pH to >4 in less than one minute, and this effect dissipated by 3 minutes. This is a secondary summary of the original paper, and the subjects were fasted. https://nutritionbyerin.com/acid-reflux-mythbusters-drinking-water-dilutes-stomach-acid/
3. **Food is the acid trigger.** The fact-check notes that the study participants weren't eating, and that food is a signal for the stomach to produce gastric acid. https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/fact-check/drinking-water-meals-dilutes-juices-enough-affect-digestion/
4. **Parietal cells compensate.** A gastroenterologist's explainer says parietal cells respond by producing more acid to compensate. This comes from a Medium post, so treat it as expert commentary rather than primary evidence. https://medium.com/@gastro.doc.co/does-drinking-water-with-meals-really-dilute-stomach-acid-lets-settle-this-once-and-for-all-56af95794fa2

**About 10–30 minutes**
5. **The "Magenstrasse" (stomach road).** Water drunk after a solid meal takes a fast lane. In an MRI study, gastric emptying of the ingested water was rapid and usually completed after approximately 20 min, regardless of the applied volume. The volumes were 50, 100 or 150 mL after a light meal. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12320173/
6. **Water leaves much faster than food.** The normal half-emptying time for noncaloric liquids such as water is 10-15 minutes, versus about 77 minutes +/- 32 minutes for a solid meal. https://www.auntminnie.com/scott-williams/nuclear-medicine/gastrointentinal/article/15561614/gastrointestinal-motility
7. **Water pools in the small intestine.** Twelve minutes after drinking, small bowel water content rose to a maximum of 94 ± 24 mL, in 15 ± 2 pockets of 6 ± 2 mL each. Gastric volume was back to baseline 45 min after the water dose in about half the subjects. https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/mp500210c
8. **Surprising mechanism.** Water bypasses the meal and mixes with it only slightly. A 19th-century BMJ note already observed that non-irritating liquids pass through the "tubular" stomach, and only mix with food to a slight extent. This is historical, but it matches the modern MRI data. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9242119/

**Minutes to hours**
9. **Absorption and kidneys.** The small intestine does most of the absorbing, since water absorption is directly linked to the movement of solutes, particularly sodium. Kidneys can start making extra urine within 15 to 60 minutes after drinking. This is a secondary source, so treat the timing as approximate. https://biologyinsights.com/how-long-after-drinking-does-it-take-to-pee/
10. **ADH switches off.** When a large volume of water is consumed, the blood becomes more diluted, which suppresses the release of ADH, so you urinate more. https://biologyinsights.com/how-long-after-drinking-does-it-take-to-pee/
11. **The kidney speed limit.** The kidneys can only remove 0.8 to 1.0 liters of water per hour. If sodium falls below 135 mmol/L, doctors call it hyponatremia. This is a danger for endurance athletes and soldiers, not for sipping with dinner. https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/318619

**The real downside: volume and reflux**
12. **Meal volume drives reflux, not acid dilution.** In 15 GERD patients, a 600 mL regimen had more reflux episodes (17 ± 4 vs 10 ± 2) than a 300 mL regimen, because larger meals distend the gastric fundus. These were liquid meals, not plain water. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24712047/
13. **Drinking with meals and GERD.** A population study found that "eating rate" and "intra-meal fluid intake" were not significantly associated with GERD. A shorter meal-to-sleep interval was linked to GERD (the long interval had an OR of 0.73). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317853171_Total_diet_individual_meals_and_their_association_with_gastroesophageal_reflux_disease

**Days to months**
14. **Pre-meal water (not post-meal) and weight, 12 weeks.** In a randomized trial of 84 adults with obesity, participants drank 500 ml of water 30 min before their main meals. They lost 1.3 kg more than comparators at follow up. The authors call this preliminary evidence. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26237305/
15. **Dose-response (observational subgroup).** People who preloaded before all three meals lost 4.3 kg over the 12 weeks, versus 0.8 kg for those who preloaded once or not at all. This is a self-reported subgroup, not a randomized comparison. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/08/150826101645.htm
16. **Older dieters.** An earlier trial of 48 adults found the water group lost about 2 kg more after 12 weeks. A 2024 meta-analysis reportedly found almost nothing, which is a secondary claim worth verifying before use. https://getfitcraft.com/blog/does-drinking-water-help-lose-weight

**Baseline hydration facts**
17. **Daily targets.** Mayo Clinic gives 11.5 cups (women) and 15.5 cups (men) of total fluid, and about 20% comes from food. https://www.mayoclinichealthsystem.org/hometown-health/speaking-of-health/tips-for-drinking-more-water
18. **Mayo's practical advice.** It suggests drinking water regularly with meals, throughout exercising and when you feel thirsty. A quick check is that your urine is colorless or light yellow. https://www.mayoclinichealthsystem.org/hometown-health/speaking-of-health/tips-for-drinking-more-water

## Myths to bust, with the evidence
- **"Water dilutes stomach acid and ruins digestion."** Any dilution is transient (about 3 minutes in fasted subjects), and eating triggers more acid. The Rappler fact-check rates the claim FALSE. https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/fact-check/drinking-water-meals-dilutes-juices-enough-affect-digestion/
- **"Water flushes food through too fast."** Studies have shown liquids don't impact how quickly food empties from your stomach. This is from a lifestyle site, but it is consistent with the Magenstrasse data above. https://nutritiouslife.com/drink-up/drink-water-during-meals/
- **A historical angle.** The same debate ran in 1880–1891: in 1888 the BMJ said it was more than doubtful whether any such effect is in reality produced. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9474040/
