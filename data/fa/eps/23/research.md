## Timeline: what happens in the body

**Seconds to minutes**
- **Calcium and CaMKII fire first.** A strong signal opens the NMDA receptor, calcium floods in, and the enzyme CaMKII switches on. The cellular storage of a new memory starts in seconds, though this comes from a non-peer-reviewed explainer. [neurosity.co](https://neurosity.co/guides/long-term-potentiation-biology-memory)
- **Spines swell within minutes.** In a model of LTP (the cellular mechanism of learning), the volume of the spine increases by 150% within minutes. [Nature Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08459-0)
- **The cell's scaffolding is rebuilt quickly.** The actin rebuilding that enlarges the spine has mostly decayed after around 20-30 minutes. [Nature Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08459-0)
- **Extra spines sprout, then vanish.** Transient increases in dendritic spine density are detectable thirty minutes after LTP induction. By sixty minutes, spine density has returned to basal levels. [PMC4432235](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4432235/)
- **Short-term memory doesn't need new proteins.** Mice lacking CREB had normal short-term memory lasting between 30 and 60 min but were profoundly deficient in long-term memory. [Bourtchuladze/Silva, via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13367875_Consolidation_of_Memory_After_its_Reactivation_Involvement_of_ss_Noradrenergic_Receptors_in_the_Late_Phase)

**Hours**
- **A "synaptic tag" has a deadline.** The synaptic tag decays in less than three hours. The cell has to deliver proteins to the tagged synapse before then. [Cell, 2002](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0092-8674(02)00657-8)
- **Two phases of LTP.** A short-term early potentiation lasting 1 to 3 hours, which is independent of protein synthesis, precedes a later, longer lasting stage (L-LTP), which requires protein synthesis. [ResearchGate summary of Cell paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11470744_Expression_of_Constitutively_Active_CREB_Protein_Facilitates_the_Late_Phase_of_Long-Term_Potentiation_by_Enhancing_Synaptic_Capture)
- **Genes switch on.** CREB, a transcription factor, activates genes for new proteins about 30–60 minutes after a strong signal, per the same explainer. [neurosity.co](https://neurosity.co/guides/long-term-potentiation-biology-memory)
- **Surprising second wave at about 12 hours.** In rats, 12 hr after acquisition of a one-trial associative learning task, there is a novel protein synthesis and BDNF-dependent phase in the hippocampus that is critical for the persistence of LTM storage. Blocking it didn't hurt memory at 2 days but did at 7 days. [Neuron, 2007](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0896-6273(06)01026-9)
- **Spines double.** In rat slices, dendritic spine density more than doubled 2 hr following theta-burst stimulation. [PMC6728136](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6728136/)
- **One quick caveat on the same finding:** learning did not increase the total number of synapses in one rat study. The change was in which spines were activated, not the total count. [J Neurosci 2007](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/27/30/8031)

**First night's sleep**
- **Your hippocampus replays the day.** The hippocampus is thought to "teach" this information to the neocortex over time, especially during sleep. [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2123432119)
- **The replay machinery is three rhythms working together.** These are hippocampal sharp wave ripples, thalamocortical spindles, and neocortical slow oscillations. [bioRxiv/PNAS model](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.01.31.478475.full.pdf)
- **The replay has been seen in humans.** Researchers recorded whole-night EEG from the hippocampus and neocortex in 14 pre-surgical epilepsy patients. [PMC7363445](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7363445/)
- **Sleep can even reverse forgetting.** A 2015 replication of the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve found "a jump upwards starting at the 24 hour data point", which some authors attribute to sleep. [Murre & Dros 2015, summarised here](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/edtech-growth/spaced-repetition-forgetting-curve)

**Days**
- **Forgetting is steep at first.** In Ebbinghaus's data, relearning after 20 minutes saved 58.2 percent of the original work, while relearning after 31 days still saved 21.1 percent. [Buttondown summary](https://buttondown.com/academicinfo/archive/infographic-of-the-forgetting-curve-and-spaced/)
- **Spacing beats cramming.** Distributed practice outperformed massed practice by 10–30% in a review of 184 articles and 317 experiments (Cepeda 2006), according to a secondary source. [flashcardify](https://www.flashcardify.me/blog/ebbinghaus-forgetting-curve)
- **Why cramming fails at the synapse.** A repeat that comes too soon cannot further affect spines in the spaced-learning model. Revisiting hours later hits a primed synapse and can trigger protein synthesis. [arXiv review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.08370)
- **White matter can change in hours.** One study reported changes in the fornix as quickly as after a few hours of spatial learning in a car racing game. [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.10.02.324004.full.pdf)

**Weeks**
- **New myelin is needed to learn.** In mice, young adult mice need to make myelin to learn new motor skills but new myelin does not need to be produced to recall and perform a pre-learned skill. [UCL / Science 2014](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2014/oct/myelin-vital-learning-new-practical-skills)
- **Mice that couldn't make new myelin failed at the task.** Blocking new oligodendrocytes prevented the mice from mastering the complex wheel. [Science/PMC6324726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6324726/)
- **Human white matter after 6 weeks of juggling.** Changes appeared in the white matter under the intraparietal sulcus after 6 weeks, as reviewed in the same bioRxiv paper. [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.10.02.324004.full.pdf)
- **Four weeks of balance training in 24 adults.** MRI showed predominantly myelin-related changes across distributed motor fiber tracts. [Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-026-09712-w)

**Months**
- **Juggling grows grey matter.** After three months, the jugglers had a 3% increase in the volume of grey matter in the mid-temporal part of the brain. [ABC Science on Draganski, Nature 2004](https://www.abc.net.au/science/articles/2004/01/22/1029268.htm)
- **Use it or lose some of it.** Three months after the jugglers stopped juggling the 3% increase had fallen to a 2% increase. [ABC Science](https://www.abc.net.au/science/articles/2004/01/22/1029268.htm)
- **Same effect in older adults.** In people aged 50–67, increases appeared in the same visual-motion area and reversed after the three months without practice. [studyx summary of Boyke 2008](https://www.studyx.app/devlog/what-is-neuroplasticity)

**Years**
- **London taxi drivers.** The study compared them with bus drivers who drive the same city. Taxi drivers had greater gray matter volume in mid-posterior hippocampi and less volume in anterior hippocampi. [Maguire 2006, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17024677/)
- **There is a trade-off.** The ability to acquire new visuo-spatial information was worse in taxi drivers than in bus drivers. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17024677/)
- **Dose-response.** Years of experience correlated with posterior hippocampal volume (r = 0.72, P = 0.002), per the Academia.edu summary. [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/68240013/London_Taxi_Drivers_and_Bus_Drivers_a_Structural_MRI_and_Neuropsychological_Analysis)
- **The training is brutal.** "The Knowledge" involves a series of grueling exams that only about 50 percent of hopefuls pass. [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/london-taxi-memory/)

## Surprising moments for the video
- **New neurons are a small contributor.** In adult humans, 700 new neurons are added in each hippocampus per day, an annual turnover of 1.75% (Spalding, Cell 2013). [Cell](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0092-8674(13)00533-3)
- **That figure is tiny in context.** It is less than 0.03% of the neurons in an adult hippocampus. [Science news](https://www.science.org/content/article/genetic-evidence-our-brains-make-new-neurons-adulthood-may-close-century-old-debate)
- **The debate isn't fully closed.** Some studies have reported essentially no detectable neurogenesis in adult humans beyond childhood, while a 2025 Science study identified proliferating progenitors in the adult human hippocampus. [Exploration review](https://www.explorationpub.com/Journals/en/Article/1006128), [Science 2025](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adu9575)
- **A memory trace can be switched off.** Inducing long-term depression (LTD) and long-term potentiation (LTP) in the neuronal network modified during learning impairs and reactivates memory, respectively. [PMC12864489](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12864489/)

## Myths to bust
- **"You forget 70% in 24 hours."** This is a popular line, but the source warns, "Treat any universal percentage with suspicion." Ebbinghaus tested only himself, on nonsense syllables. [rework.com](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/edtech-growth/spaced-repetition-forgetting-curve), [Buttondown](https://buttondown.com/academicinfo/archive/infographic-of-the-forgetting-curve-and-spaced/)
- **"Learning grows your brain permanently."** The juggling gains partly regressed after practice stopped (see Months above).
- **"Grey matter growth means new neurons."** The juggling researchers suggested the cause was neurons extending and making more connections with other neurons, and they couldn't explain the later decrease. [ABC Science](https://www.abc.net.au/science/articles/2004/01/22/1029268.htm)
- **"The juggling effect is about motor skill."** The authors concluded that the visual-spatial demands of juggling mattered more than the motor skills. [ABC Science](https://www.abc.net.au/science/articles/2004/01/22/1029268.htm)

## Everyday hooks
- **Cramming the night before an exam.** This produces early LTP only, which fades within hours because it never triggers the protein synthesis needed for late LTP (explainer source, so treat as a simplified model). [neurosity.co](https://neurosity.co/guides/long-term-potentiation-biology-memory)
- **A good night's sleep after learning.** The overnight replay described above is the biological reason it helps.
- **Giving up a hobby.** The juggling reversal shows that gains fade without practice.
