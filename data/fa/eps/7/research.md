## Timeline: what happens after you eat sugar

**Seconds to minutes**
1. **Mouth, seconds to 5 minutes.** Within seconds of sugar exposure, oral bacteria begin metabolizing it and producing lactic acid. Plaque pH falls from a resting value of about 7.0 to roughly 5.0 within a couple of minutes. Sources: Stephan curve explainer https://www.dentagum.co/blogs/news/stephan-curve-explained and https://www.innovasmilesdental.com/blog/how-sugar-damages-teeth *(dental-practice sources, not WHO/NIH)*.
2. **Enamel threshold.** Enamel starts to demineralize when the pH at the tooth surface falls below about 5.5. The acidic environment persists for 20–60 minutes. That makes sipping a soda for an hour much worse than drinking it quickly: with frequent snacking or sipping, your mouth never gets a chance to recover. https://toothcheck.io/blog/mouth-ph-and-oral-health and https://dentrends.com/denthink/how-sugar-affects-teeth-scientifically/
3. **Myth-buster for teeth.** Sugar itself does not directly damage teeth. It fuels bacteria that make acid. The WHO also ties free sugars above 10% of energy to more cavities: rates of dental caries are higher when free sugars intake is more than 10% of total calories. https://www.paho.org/en/news/4-3-2015-paho-and-who-urge-countries-reduce-sugar-consumption-among-adults-and-children
4. **Gut to blood, 10–20 minutes.** In a metabolically healthy person, blood sugar begins to rise within 10-15 minutes of starting to eat. https://levlaenge.tech/en/articles/sugar-and-insulin-after-a-meal-what-happens-in-the-body-and-what-is-normal/

**30–90 minutes**
5. **Peak and insulin's two waves.** In a healthy person, blood glucose typically peaks within about 30 minutes of consuming pure sugar. Pancreatic beta cells release insulin in two phases: first a quick pulse of stored insulin, then a slower, sustained release to help muscles and fat cells absorb glucose. Insulin is the only hormone your body makes that can lower blood sugar. https://scienceinsights.org/what-happens-when-you-have-a-sugar-rush/
6. **Normal numbers for the graphic.** Blood sugar peaks within about an hour and stays below 140 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L) two hours after the first bite, drifting back toward a fasting level of 70 to 99 mg/dL. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/blood-sugar-levels-after-eating-normal-ranges-spikes-and-what-your-numbers-mean/
7. **A built-in safety valve.** If blood sugar climbs above roughly 180 mg/dL, the kidneys start filtering excess glucose into the urine. https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-flush-sugar-out-of-your-body-timelines/ *(the URL as listed is https://scienceinsights.org/how-long-to-flush-sugar-out-of-your-body-timelines/)*
8. **A 10-minute walk helps.** In a Scientific Reports study, a 10-minute walk right after consuming glucose lowered peak blood sugar from about 182 to 164 mg/dL compared with sitting still. https://scienceinsights.org/how-long-to-flush-sugar-out-of-your-body-timelines/

**1–3 hours**
9. **The "sugar crash".** The crash tends to hit between one and three hours after eating. A large amount of sugar on an empty stomach makes a steep spike, and the insulin response can overshoot, pulling blood sugar lower than where it started. This matches the 3 pm slump and the mid-morning doughnut crash. https://scienceinsights.org/what-happens-when-you-have-a-sugar-rush/
10. **The brain's reward hit.** Sugar activates central reward pathways and, like drugs of abuse, stimulates dopamine release. There is a catch: one study found no significant difference in brain chemical levels between sucrose and equally sweet artificial sweetener solutions. So the taste of sweetness matters, not only the calories. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7689136/ and https://scienceinsights.org/does-sugar-release-endorphins-and-dopamine/

## Myths to bust
11. **"Sugar makes kids hyperactive" is false.** A JAMA meta-analysis by Wolraich, Wilson and White pooled 23 double-blind, placebo-controlled trials and concluded sugar does not affect children's behavior or cognition. In a 1994 study, mothers told their children had received sugar, when all had received placebo, rated them as more hyperactive. The effect was in the parents' expectations. https://www.brain-zone.net/learn/brain-health/nutrition/how-sugar-affects-brain and https://psychologybybogdanova.com/publications/sugar-and-the-brain-glucose-energy-reward-and-common-myths/
12. **"Sugar addiction" is debated.** A review argues the lack of reproducibility of fMRI data makes it problematic to treat shared brain pathways as conclusive evidence for sugar addiction. Another source notes that there is no universally established sugar withdrawal timeline. Symptoms can also be caused by reduced calorie intake, hunger, poor sleep, dehydration, or caffeine withdrawal. https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/18/9791 and https://thriveinserenity.com/sugar-withdrawal-timeline/

## Weeks of eating a lot of sugar
13. **The liver is the fructose processor.** Fructose is almost entirely cleared by the liver, and its metabolism there stimulates lipogenesis (fat-making). https://hbsn.amegroups.org/article/view/5213/html
14. **A 7-week randomized trial in the Journal of Hepatology.** Healthy lean men drank fructose-, sucrose- or glucose-sweetened drinks (80 g/day) on top of their usual diet. Fructose and sucrose roughly doubled the liver's basal output of newly synthesized fatty acids compared with control (about 20% per day vs 9.1%). Glucose did not. https://www.journal-of-hepatology.eu/article/S0168-8278(21)00161-6/fulltext and https://drperlmutter.com/study/fructose-and-sucrose-but-not-glucose-sweetened-beverages-promote-hepatic-de-novo-lipogenesis-a-randomized-controlled-trial/
15. **Long-term cardiovascular risk (CDC authors, JAMA Internal Medicine 2014).** Compared with those eating under 10% of calories as added sugar, people at 10–24.9% had an adjusted hazard ratio of 1.30 for CVD death, and those at 25% or more had 2.75. Most adults consumed 10% or more of calories from added sugar (71.4%), and about 10% consumed 25% or more. This is an observational association, not proof of cause. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/150399

## Quitting: the surprising fast wins
16. **9 days is enough to see changes in kids.** In a Gastroenterology study, as few as 9 days of isocaloric fructose restriction significantly reduced liver fat, de novo lipogenesis and visceral fat, and improved insulin sensitivity, secretion and clearance in children with obesity and metabolic syndrome. The UC press release reported diastolic blood pressure down 5 mm, triglycerides down 33 points, LDL down 10 points, and insulin levels cut by one-third. The study kept calories the same and swapped starch for sugar. Caveat: critics noted it was an uncontrolled before–after study, and the authors reply that the children lost only 0.9 kg, mostly in the first 4 days. https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/s0016-5085(17)35685-8/fulltext, https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/cutting-sugar-kids-diets-improves-health-just-days and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/oby.21371
17. **What quitting feels like (low-quality sources, treat as anecdotal).** Popular guides put the worst of it at days 3–5, with acute symptoms such as headaches, fatigue and irritability lasting 5 to 10 days. They add that cravings typically diminish significantly by the end of week two. The "months" claims, such as a full neurological reset in 2–3 months, come from blog sources without clinical backing. https://berrypure.app/sugar-withdrawal-timeline and https://sugarexit.com/sugar-withdrawal-timeline-day-by-day-what-to-expect

## Reference numbers for on-screen text
- **WHO guideline:** free sugars should be under 10% of total energy intake, and below 5% would provide additional health benefits. Below 5% is roughly 25 grams (6 teaspoons) per day. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-NMH-NHD-15.3 and https://apps.who.int/mediacentre/news/releases/2015/sugar-guideline/en/index.html
- **Hidden sugar:** 1 tablespoon of ketchup has about 4 g of free sugars, and a single can of soda up to 40 g (about 10 teaspoons). One can therefore exceeds the whole 25 g ideal limit.
- **Fruit exception:** the WHO guideline does not refer to sugars in fresh fruits and vegetables or naturally in milk, because there is no reported evidence of adverse effects.

## Gaps to know about
- I found no WHO, CDC, NHS or Mayo Clinic page giving a minute-by-minute timeline, so the glucose and insulin timing comes from secondary health sites. They agree with standard physiology.
- Quitting-timeline claims beyond about 2 weeks lack clinical trial support. For "weeks and months," the safest on-screen claims are the 9-day liver and lipid data and the WHO and CVD risk figures.
