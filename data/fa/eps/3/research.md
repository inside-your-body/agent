## Part 1: The "falling" jolt (seconds to minutes)

- **The jolt itself.** A hypnic jerk is a sudden, involuntary muscle twitch as you drift off. It often feels like a jolt or a falling sensation. [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/hypnic-jerks)
- **How common it is.** About 70% of people have experienced a hypnic jerk. This comes from Wikipedia, so check it against a primary source. [Wikipedia: Falling (sensation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_(sensation))
- **Where it happens in the body.** A misfire is suspected between nerves in the reticular brainstem. [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/hypnic-jerks)
- **The "brain is fooled" theory.** The brain may read rapid muscle relaxation as a real loss of balance. That would make it mistake relaxation for an actual fall. The exact cause is not fully understood. [Naturepedic](https://www.naturepedic.com/blogs/sleep-guides/hypnotic-jerks)
- **The tree-ancestor theory.** One researcher suggested it is "an archaic reflex" in which the brain misreads muscle relaxation as a sleeping primate falling out of a tree. This is a hypothesis, not proven. [Wikipedia: Hypnic jerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnic_jerk)
- **Why you also "see" a fall.** Sleep onset is a twilight state where waking awareness and dream-like imagery overlap, so many people also get a short "falling dream" at the same moment. [Naturepedic](https://www.naturepedic.com/blogs/sleep-guides/hypnotic-jerks)
- **It happens in the lightest sleep.** A hypnic jerk occurs during the non-REM sleep cycle, with an "illusion of falling." [Wikipedia citing the Journal of Neural Transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnic_jerk)
- **Everyday triggers viewers will recognise.** Fatigue, stress, sleep deprivation, vigorous exercise, and stimulants like caffeine and nicotine can trigger them. [PMC10590197](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10590197/)
- **Surprising twist: being overtired makes it worse.** Ironically, being overly tired can increase hypnic jerks. Worrying about them can also raise their frequency, which creates a loop. [Ubie Health](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/why-feel-falling-before-sleep-hypnic-jerk-47-brain32e4)
- **Myth check: it is not a seizure.** A hypnic jerk differs from a myoclonic seizure because it occurs at sleep onset only and the EEG is normal. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnic_jerk)
- **When it becomes a problem.** Hypnic jerks are usually benign, but they can intensify and interfere with sleep onset, causing insomnia. [PMC10590197](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10590197/)

## Part 2: Falling asleep (minutes to hours)

- **Two drives push you under.** Adenosine, a by-product of neural activity, builds up through the day and suppresses arousal circuits in the basal forebrain. The brain's body clock (the suprachiasmatic nucleus) then triggers melatonin release from the pineal gland, which signals that the sleep window has opened. [Sound Health](https://soundhealth.life/blogs/sleep/neuroscience-of-sleep-onset)
- **Caffeine blocks the first drive.** Caffeine and related compounds are adenosine antagonists that increase wakefulness. [Brainscape](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/insomnia-8378344/packs/14013675) The Yale source explains the buildup: the longer you're awake, the more adenosine accumulates and the more sleep pressure you have. [Yale Medicine](https://medicine.yale.edu/internal-medicine/pulmonary/news/national-sleep-week/good-sleep-recipe/)
- **Your core cools while your hands and feet warm.** At sleep onset core temperature drops while skin temperature rises. Melatonin increases heat loss at the extremities and lowers core temperature, followed by greater sleepiness. [Neurolaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/does-your-body-temperature-drop-when-you-sleep/), [PMC2817387](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817387/figure/fig3)
- **How big the drop is.** One source says it is 1 to 2°F overall, reaching its lowest point around 4 to 5 AM. Another says changes around sleep onset are measured in tenths of a degree, not whole degrees. Both can be true, because the overall nightly fall is larger than the change at the moment of sleep onset. [Superpower](https://superpower.com/guides/sleep-health/does-your-body-temperature-rise-when-you-sleep), [Dr Kumar](https://drkumardiscovery.com/posts/sleep-thermoregulation/)
- **Everyday tie-in: the hot shower.** A hot shower before bed may help because it dissipates body heat and cools the body. [Yale Medicine](https://medicine.yale.edu/internal-medicine/pulmonary/news/national-sleep-week/good-sleep-recipe/)
- **Stage timings in one cycle.** N1 lasts up to 5 minutes, and N2 lasts around 25 minutes in the first cycle. In N2 the brain becomes substantially insulated from external stimuli. [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sleep-cycle-stages), [Sound Health](https://soundhealth.life/blogs/sleep/neuroscience-of-sleep-onset)
- **The 90-minute rhythm.** A cycle runs roughly 90 to 120 minutes, and most people complete four to six a night. [Acibadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/sleep-cycles-explained-the-90-minute-rhythm-the-stages-and-why-waking-time-matters/)
- **Your first REM is brief.** The first REM period is about 10 minutes and occurs 90 minutes after falling asleep. In the final cycle REM can last up to 1 hour. [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/sleep-stages.html), [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sleep-cycle-stages)
- **REM paralysis.** Temporary muscle paralysis occurs naturally in REM and prevents you acting out dreams. It is the opposite of the hypnic jerk, which happens before the paralysis sets in. [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sleep-cycle-stages)
- **Deep sleep comes first, REM later.** Early cycles are dominated by slow-wave deep sleep and later cycles by REM. That is why cutting sleep short costs you mostly REM. [The Sleep Reset](https://www.thesleepreset.com/learn/sleep-cycle)
- **Brain cleaning while you sleep (mouse data).** In Nedergaard's 2013 *Science* study, interstitial space volume increased by more than 60 percent in sleeping mice, allowing greater glymphatic flow. Beta-amyloid left sleeping mice's brains twice as fast as awake mice's. Tracer influx was reduced by 95 percent in awake mice compared with sleeping ones. [Neurology Today](https://neurologytoday.aan.com/doi/10.1097/01.NT.0000438829.62018.15), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-sleep-cleans-the-brain-and-keeps-you-healthy/)
- **The molecule behind it.** The system relies on aquaporin 4 (AQP4) water channels. Knocking out AQP4 reduced amyloid-beta clearance by 65%. [PMC3867735](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3867735/)

## Part 3: If you don't get the sleep (hours, days, weeks)

- **24 hours awake.** Effects are similar to alcohol impairment to the point where driving isn't safe. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/23970-sleep-deprivation)
- **Crash risk.** After only 4-5 hours of sleep in the last 24, motor vehicle accident risk is 5.4 times greater than after 7 or more hours. [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/sleep-deprivation-effects-symptoms/)
- **Microsleeps.** Most people start to experience microsleeps in stage 2 of sleep deprivation and have trouble thinking or focusing. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/23970-sleep-deprivation)
- **About 72 hours.** Misperceptions of real objects start around 48 hours awake and progress to full hallucinations by about 72 hours. [Acibadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep-deprivation-hour-by-hour/)
- **One night costs the brain.** A single night of sleep deprivation significantly increases soluble amyloid-beta, shown with PET imaging. This is cited in a patent filing, so find the primary paper before quoting it. Another secondary source puts the NIH PET finding at a 5% increase after one night. [USPTO filing](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12648931), [Neurosity](https://neurosity.co/guides/glymphatic-system-brain-waste-clearance)
- **One week at about 6 hours (PNAS).** The study had 26 participants, one week of insufficient sleep (5.7 hours) and one week of sufficient sleep (8.5 hours). Over 700 genes were affected, including genes linked to inflammation, immunity, and the stress response. [Sleep Education (AASM)](https://sleepeducation.org/sleep-deprivation-disrupts-genes/)
- **Body clock genes flatten.** Genes that normally rise and fall across the 24-hour day dropped from 1,855 to 1,481. The paper itself is here: [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1217154110).
- **You stop noticing the decline.** Even six hours nightly produces steadily worsening cognitive performance within two weeks, comparable to one to two full days without sleep, and people stop noticing their own decline. [Acibadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep-deprivation-hour-by-hour/)
- **Weeks to months.** Chronic deprivation can raise the risk of heart and kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, stroke, obesity, and mental health issues. Recovering from chronic sleep debt can take weeks or even months of consistently meeting your sleep need. [UPMC](https://share.upmc.com/2026/04/sleep-deprivation-effects/), [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/sleep-deprivation-effects-symptoms/)

## Myths and caveats for the script

- **"Falling jerks mean something is wrong."** Hypnic jerks are considered a normal part of falling asleep. [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/hypnic-jerks)
- **"You only dream in REM."** Dreaming can also occur in other stages. [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sleep-cycle-stages)
- **Glymphatic caveat.** One review notes that the authors have not directly shown that beta amyloid is removed specifically by CSF flow, as other pathways exist. The human evidence is still developing. [Science in the Classroom](https://www.scienceintheclassroom.org/research-papers/deep-clean-your-brain-one-easy-step)
