## Part A: What smoking does, in seconds and minutes

1. **7–20 seconds: nicotine reaches the brain.** Nicotine from cigarette smoke reaches the brain 15–20 seconds after a puff, while a less rigorous source says 7–10 seconds, faster than intravenous drugs. The sources differ, so say "under 20 seconds." Nicotine binds nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (α4β2 subtype), triggering dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958544/ (NIH/PMC); https://getburnout.app/articles/science

2. **Under 1 minute: heart rate climbs.** Within one minute of inhaling smoke, heart rate begins to rise, by as much as 30 percent during the first 10 minutes of smoking. Source: https://bio.libretexts.org/Courses/Canada_College/BIOL_130:_Human_Biology_(White)/15:_Respiratory_System/15.05:_Smoking_and_Health

3. **Surprising nuance: it's not only nicotine.** In a study reported in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology, smoking raised heart rate to about 75 bpm versus 66 bpm with carbon monoxide or air. Carbon monoxide alone had no significant effect on heart rate, blood pressure, or stress hormones. Source: https://biologyinsights.com/how-long-does-nicotine-increase-heart-rate/

4. **Minutes: carbon monoxide hijacks hemoglobin.** CO binds hemoglobin with about 200x the affinity of oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin. Hemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. This is why the first cigarette causes lightheadedness. Sources: https://getburnout.app/articles/science; https://sciencepedia.blog/what-happens-smoking-first-time

5. **Each puff is a chemical cocktail.** Each puff contains over 7,000 chemicals, and the smoke hits the lungs quickly, so the blood carries these toxins to the rest of the body. Even in low amounts, acrolein can cause a sore throat in 10 minutes. Source (FDA): https://www.fda.gov/tobacco-products/health-effects-tobacco-use/keep-your-air-clear-how-tobacco-can-harm-your-lungs

6. **Cilia get paralysed without the cells dying.** Smoke damages the tiny hair-like cilia on cell surfaces even without killing the cells. Acrolein and formaldehyde are suspected causes of this damage. The mucus then accumulates into globs, which triggers smoker's cough. Sources: https://blogs.ubc.ca/communicatingscience2017w211/2018/01/27/still-smoking-cigarettes-its-effects-on-cilia-just-might-make-you-quit/; https://www.tobaccoinaustralia.org.au/chapter-3-health-effects/3-2-respiratory-diseases; https://www.britannica.com/video/Smoking-damage-respiratory-system/-107179

7. **Everyday moment: why smokers cough in the morning.** At night the cilia regain some function without smoke, so on waking the smoker coughs up the accumulated mucus and tar. Source: the UBC link above (a student science blog, so treat it as explanatory rather than primary).

## Part B: The recovery timeline after the last cigarette

8. **20 minutes:** blood pressure and pulse rate return to normal. Source (NHS-style timeline): https://onlinedoctor.lloydspharmacy.com/uk/lifestyle-advice/stop-smoking-timeline. The American Cancer Society confirms heart rate drops within minutes: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/risk-prevention/tobacco/guide-quitting-smoking/benefits-of-quitting-smoking-over-time.html

9. **8–12 hours: carbon monoxide clears, oxygen rises.** Within 8 to 12 hours, blood CO drops and blood oxygen increases. Timing varies by source: between twelve hours (WHO) and several days (CDC), with the NHS at 48 hours. Also, after about 8 hours, nicotine levels in the blood have halved and withdrawal starts to emerge. Sources: https://www.healthline.com/health/effects-of-quitting-smoking; https://getburnout.app/articles/science

10. **24–72 hours: nicotine is essentially gone, and the hardest days hit.** Withdrawal symptoms peak around 24 hours. Nicotine is almost completely cleared by 48 hours. This is why days 2–3 feel worst: the molecule is gone but the receptors are still demanding it. At 48 hours, nerve endings damaged by smoking begin to regrow, improving smell and taste. Sources: https://smokeclock.com/blog/quit-smoking-benefits-timeline/; https://www.healthline.com/health/effects-of-quitting-smoking

11. **72 hours:** bronchial tubes start to relax, and breathing gets easier. Source: https://www.webmd.com/smoking-cessation/ss/slideshow-effects-of-quitting-smoking

12. **2 weeks to 3 months: circulation and lungs.** WHO states in 2–12 weeks, circulation improves and lung function increases. Some popular sources claim a 30% lung function gain at about 2 weeks. That figure is attributed to the American Lung Association, so cite it carefully. Sources: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/tobacco-health-benefits-of-smoking-cessation; https://www.healthline.com/health/what-happens-when-you-quit-smoking

13. **1–9 months to years: cilia regrow, then long-term risks fall.**
   - Cilia regrow within 1 to 9 months. This comes from a secondary source, so treat it as approximate.
   - WHO: at 1 year, coronary heart disease risk is about half a smoker's. Stroke risk equals a nonsmoker's 5–15 years after quitting.
   - At 10 years, lung cancer risk is about half a smoker's. At 15 years, coronary heart disease risk equals a nonsmoker's.
   - Quitting at about 30 gains almost 10 years of life expectancy. Quitting at about 60 gains 3 years.
   - Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/q-a-detail/health-benefits-of-smoking-cessation

## Part C: Milk, and the myths

14. **What milk actually does for smokers is change the taste.** In a Duke study (Nicotine and Tobacco Research, April 2007), 209 adults were asked which foods affected cigarette taste. 45% reported foods or drinks that made cigarettes taste worse, with milk mentioned by 19%. The effect is self-reported taste, not a measured chemical one. Alcohol, coffee and meat enhanced the taste. Sources: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/04/070404174336.htm; http://www.irishhealth.com/article.html?id=11422

15. **Myth: milk "detoxes" or flushes nicotine or tar.** I found no credible evidence for this. The claim appears only on low-quality blogs. Nicotine clearance runs on liver metabolism and the half-life, which one source puts at about two hours. The Duke researchers themselves recommend diet tweaks in combination with standard nicotine replacement therapy. Source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/04/070404174336.htm

16. **Myth: milk makes mucus or phlegm.** A review in *Archives of Disease in Childhood* found studies back to 1948 show milk is not associated with more respiratory mucus. The sensation is real but misleading: milk mixes with saliva and feels more viscous, which feels like more mucus but is just milk emulsion lingering in the throat. A randomized trial in children also tested 240 ml of cow's milk against soy milk: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7488715/. Sources: https://www.livescience.com/63517-milk-does-not-create-mucus.html; https://www.sciencefocus.com/the-human-body/does-drinking-milk-create-phlegm. Smokers' phlegm comes from damaged cilia, not milk (see fact 6).

17. **Milk will not repair smoking damage, and the lung data is mixed.**
   - An NHANES analysis found frequent childhood milk intake was linked to higher FEV1 and FVC among non-smokers.
   - A large CT-based cohort found no modification of the dairy–lung function relationship by smoking status.
   - A UK Biobank Mendelian randomization study suggested milk may have a protective effect on hay fever and asthma but a negative effect on lung function.
   - On lung cancer, a prospective study found an inverse association with fermented dairy, especially among heavy smokers, but a higher risk with whole milk preference (1.24).
   - Takeaway: these are only observational associations, and none show that milk offsets smoking.
   - Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13710669; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3616890/; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357553965_Association_of_milk_intake_with_hay_fever_asthma_and_lung_function_a_Mendelian_randomization_analysis; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2405457723001845
