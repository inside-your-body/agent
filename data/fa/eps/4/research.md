"Sleeping right after eating" here means lying flat soon after a meal. Most hard data concern reflux, blood sugar and sleep quality, so the "days, weeks, months" section rests on association studies, not proven cause-and-effect. I flag where evidence is weak.

## MINUTES

1. **Acid pocket forms within about 15 minutes.** After a meal, a layer of unbuffered acid collects in the upper stomach.
   - The median onset of this "acid pocket" was 14 minutes, and in one case it was first seen at 17 minutes and grew more acidic until 44 minutes.
   - It is the reservoir for acid reflux in healthy individuals and GERD patients.
   - Source: Am J Gastroenterol review: https://www.ovid.com/jnls/ajg/fulltext/10.1038/ajg.2013.132~the-acid-pocket-a-target-for-treatment-in-reflux-disease
   - Surprising twist: using scintigraphy, researchers localized the acid pocket 30 min after a meal, aspirated it, and saw it reappear 10 min later. The stomach keeps refilling it.

2. **The acid pocket sits right at the esophagus entrance.**
   - After a meal, the acid zone was found about 2 cm more proximal than under fasting conditions, so it reaches the esophagus junction itself.
   - Source: Gastroenterology: https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(01)10822-X/fulltext

3. **The valve opens on its own after eating.** An increase in transient lower esophageal sphincter relaxations (TLESRs) after meals is a well-established mechanism for postprandial reflux in both healthy individuals and GERD patients.
   - Stretch sensors in the upper stomach trigger it: activation of gastric mechanoreceptors after the meal is the most important triggering mechanism of TLESRs.
   - Sources: https://www.ovid.com/jnls/ajg/fulltext/10.1038/ajg.2013.132~the-acid-pocket-a-target-for-treatment-in-reflux-disease and https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11894-005-0033-x

4. **Early dumping window (10–30 minutes).** Faster-than-normal emptying can cause diarrhea, bloating or nausea within 30 minutes after eating.
   - Source (NIH MedlinePlus): https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/gastric-emptying-tests/

## HOURS

5. **The stomach is still working for about 4 hours.** A mixed meal begins to enter the duodenum in about 30 minutes and requires about 4 hours to leave the stomach completely.
   - Normal upper limits of food still in the stomach are 90% at 1 hour, 60% at 2 hours, 30% at 3 hours, and 10% at 4 hours.
   - This is why "wait 3 hours" is not arbitrary.
   - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/stomach-emptying
   - Also (NIH MedlinePlus): around four hours for 90 percent of the food to move out of your stomach. https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/gastric-emptying-tests/

6. **Gravity stops helping.** When you are lying down, the contents of your stomach can push against the valve between the esophagus and stomach (lower esophageal sphincter).
   - When you're lying down, less gravity helps move food through your digestive system.
   - Sources: https://ukhealthcare.uky.edu/digestive-health-program/conditions/heartburn and https://www.healthline.com/health/gerd/improve-sleep

7. **Even healthy people reflux when lying down after a meal.** In a 1994 study, volunteers had acid reflux within the 4 hours after a meal while lying down, and they were healthy people with no reflux disease.
   - Source: https://drkumardiscovery.com/posts/body-position-affects-recumbent-postprandial-reflux/ (a secondary summary of the original paper; verify on PubMed before quoting it in the video).

8. **Which side you lie on matters.** Mayo Clinic advises that when you go to bed, start by lying on your left side to help make it less likely to have reflux.
   - In healthy volunteers, lying on the right side roughly doubled total reflux time after a high-fat meal.
   - Sources: https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/gerd/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20361959 and https://drkumardiscovery.com/posts/body-position-affects-recumbent-postprandial-reflux/

9. **The 2-hours-vs-6-hours trial.** In a randomized crossover trial, GERD patients eating 2 hours before bed had significantly more supine acid reflux than with the same meal 6 hours before bed. The effect was significant (P = 0.002).
   - Effects were largest in those who were overweight or had a hiatal hernia or esophagitis.
   - Sources: https://www.droracle.ai/articles/1119110/is-consuming-a-large-or-heavy-meal-immediately-before and https://drkumardiscovery.com/posts/nocturnal-reflux-episodes-following-administration-standardized-meal-timing/ (secondary summaries; the original trial is on PubMed).

10. **Melatonin collides with your dinner, and blood sugar suffers.** Melatonin typically rises around 2 hours before bedtime.
    - In a randomized crossover trial, an oral glucose test 1 hour before bed was compared with one 4 hours before bed. Melatonin was 3.5-fold higher in the late condition, with 6.7% lower insulin AUC and 8.3% higher glucose AUC.
    - Source (Diabetes Care, 2022): https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article-abstract/45/3/512/139258/Interplay-of-Dinner-Timing-and-MTNR1B-Type-2
    - Mechanism: genotype differences were attributed to reductions in beta-cell function (the insulin-making cells). Carriers of the MTNR1B G allele were hit harder: https://saxena.mgh.harvard.edu/news/eating-dinner-late-ups-diabetes-risk-melatonin-involved/
    - This applies to about a third of the population in the industrialized world who consume food close to bedtime. https://lactobacto.com/2022/02/09/dining-early-is-healthier-than-close-to-bedtime/

11. **Late dumping at 1–3 hours.** Symptoms such as feeling light-headed, tired, or a fast heartbeat within one to three hours after eating may point to late dumping syndrome.
    - Source (NIH MedlinePlus): https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/gastric-emptying-tests/

## THE NIGHT

12. **Sleep quality drops.** A study of 379 midlife women found that later mealtimes and less time between the last meal and bedtime were both independently associated with taking longer to fall asleep and lower sleep quality.
    - Source (PMC review): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12430387/
    - The Sleep Foundation says eating within three hours of your bedtime increases the likelihood of sleep disruptions. https://www.sleepfoundation.org/physical-health/sleep-and-overeating
    - High-fat late meals are linked to reduced deep sleep, but that source is a blog, so treat it as lower confidence.

13. **Reflux is common at night.** Approximately 80% of people with GERD experience symptoms at night.
    - Source (Healthline, citing NIDDK guidance): https://www.healthline.com/health/gerd/improve-sleep

## DAYS TO WEEKS

14. **A reflux habit builds.** A systematic review found that sitting or walking after a meal instead of lying down was negatively correlated with GERD, and less than 3 hours between dinner and sleep was positively correlated with GERD.
    - One study in that review gave an odds ratio of 1.81 (1.37–2.4) for lying down soon after eating.
    - Source (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8055252/

15. **Short-term improvement is possible.** In habitual late eaters with type 2 diabetes, a short early-dinner trial showed that bedtime hunger increased significantly on Days 4 and 5 but returned to baseline by Day 10.
    - This is a nice "your body adapts in about 10 days" moment.
    - Source (PMC): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11144034/

## MONTHS TO YEARS (association, not proven for a single habit)

16. **Chronic acid damage to the esophagus.** NIDDK says without treatment, GERD can sometimes cause serious complications over time, such as esophagitis, esophageal stricture, and Barrett's esophagus.
    - Barrett's esophagus is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of your intestine replaces the tissue lining your esophagus.
    - Source (NIDDK): https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/definition-facts

17. **Cancer risk: keep it accurate.**
    - Approximately 10% of patients with reflux develop Barrett esophagus. Esophageal adenocarcinoma occurs in 5% of patients with Barrett esophagus.
    - Source (StatPearls/NCBI): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK554462/
    - A JAMA review concludes that the risk of cancer in any given individual with GERD is low. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/194842
    - The American Cancer Society adds that most of the people who have GERD do not go on to develop esophageal cancer. https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/esophagus-cancer/causes-risks-prevention/risk-factors.html
    - Don't frame this as "sleeping after meals gives you cancer."

## MYTHS AND NUANCES

18. **Weight-gain myth.**
    - The Sleep Foundation says consuming most of a person's daily food intake late in the day can also lead to obesity, but also that the relationship between eating near bedtime and obesity is complicated. https://www.sleepfoundation.org/nutrition/is-it-bad-to-eat-before-bed
    - Examine.com says the evidence on nighttime eating and weight gain is mixed. https://examine.com/faq/does-eating-at-night-make-it-more-likely-to-gain-weight/
    - "You gain weight because your metabolism sleeps" is oversimplified.

19. **Exception: lying down can help some people.** For postprandial hypotension, which causes lightheadedness after meals, you should consider lying down for an hour after eating.
    - Source: https://www.healthline.com/health/lying-down-after-eating

20. **Official wait times** (use these as the video's "rule"):
    - Mayo Clinic: wait at least three hours after eating before lying down or going to bed. https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/gerd/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20361959
    - Kaiser Permanente: wait 2 to 3 hours before you lie down. https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/health-wellness/health-encyclopedia/he.gerd-controlling-heartburn-by-changing-your-habits.ut1339
    - NIH MedlinePlus lists waiting at least two hours after eating before lying down.
    - Mayo's raised-bed tip: raise the head end by 6 to 9 inches.

- A U.S. patent text mentioned that lying on the left side reduces acid exposure, but it is not peer-reviewed. Rely on the Mayo Clinic guidance instead.
- I found no strong evidence for "days, weeks, months" effects of one single habit. Frame those parts as risk associations.
