**HOW A NAIL GROWS (the inside story)**

1. **Growth is only 3.47 mm/month for fingernails, against 1.62 mm for toenails.** A study of healthy US young adults found fingernails grew over twice as fast as toenails (P < 0.01). https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=112380&ver=4

2. **Daily scale (about 0.1 mm/day):** that is about one-tenth of a millimeter per day, roughly the thickness of a piece of paper. https://biologyinsights.com/how-fast-do-fingernails-grow-the-science-of-nail-growth/

3. **Only the root is alive.** The matrix is the only viable component of the nail and the only structure supported by systemic circulation. https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8486426

4. **The visible nail is dead, and cutting it doesn't hurt.** The nail plate is made entirely of dead, fully keratinized cells and has no blood vessels, lymphatics or nerves. https://www.bioscience.com.pk/en/subject/biology/why-nails-grow-and-do-not-feel-pain-the-science-behind-nail-formation-and-pain-free-trimming

5. **A nail is about 25 stacked layers of dead cells.** The plate is about 0.2–0.6 mm thick and made of roughly 25 layers of dead, keratinized cells from the matrix. https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8486426

6. **The hardness comes from sulfur bonds, not calcium.** Hair-type keratins form cross-linked filament bundles stabilized by disulfide bonds between cysteine residues. Calcium is under 0.03% of the nail by weight, against 20–25% in bone. (This is a myth-buster for the "drink milk for strong nails" idea.) https://vivianandhugh.com/guide-nail-structure-and-growth.html and https://scienceinsights.org/what-are-fingernails-made-of-the-real-science/

7. **A nail is a delayed message from your matrix.** An event affecting the matrix can become visible days or weeks later, then travel toward the free edge as the nail grows. https://beautylearn.com/en/guides/nail-matrix-anatomy-growth-damage-professional-limits

8. **A full fingernail takes about 6 months.** Growth from the root to the edge of the finger takes around 6 months for fingernails and 12–18 months for toenails. https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12324680

**THE TIMELINE: WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU NEVER CUT THEM**

9. **Days 1–7: about 0.7–0.9 mm of new nail per week.** Fingernail growth works out to about 0.7 to 0.9 mm per week. That is roughly one credit-card thickness a week. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-fast-do-fingernails-grow-the-speed-of-you-measured/

10. **Around 3 mm past the fingertip, germs begin to gather.** Researchers found that artificial and natural nails longer than 3 mm beyond the fingertip carry more harmful bacteria and yeast than short nails. A fingernail reaches that length in about a month. https://abcnews.com/Health/story?id=117161&page=1

11. **Germ load in one study: all of the long-nailed workers carried bacteria and yeast, against 18% of short-nailed ones.** The study tested 18 health-care workers. The germs included Klebsiella (can cause pneumonia and urinary tract infections) and Candida parapsilosis (can cause wound and blood stream infections). https://abcnews.com/Health/story?id=117161&page=1

12. **Nuance for the "long nails are always dirtier" claim.** A study of OR nurses found no significant correlation between nail length and bacterial colony counts after a standard surgical hand scrub. Chipped nail polish, or polish worn longer than four days, increased bacteria. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326188322_Effectiveness_of_hand_hygiene_and_the_condition_of_fingernails_A_qualitative_evaluation_of_nail_microbial_colonization_following_hand_disinfection_a_pilot_study

13. **Real-life stakes: a hospital outbreak.** After an Oklahoma City hospital investigated a Pseudomonas aeruginosa outbreak in 1997–98, epidemiologists linked about half of the 16 infants who died in the neonatal unit to bacteria under long fingernails. Epidemiologists found a link but did not prove it was the cause. https://chicago.suntimes.com/well/2022/4/22/23036555/long-nails-bacteria-fungus-wellness-manicures

14. **Month 1–3: dirt under uncut nails is linked to stomach infection in kids.** H. pylori seropositivity was significantly associated with dirt under children's uncut nails (P = 0.015), but not with merely grown nails that had no dirt under them. This suggests hygiene matters more than length alone. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11027946/

15. **Months 3–6: injury risk grows with length.** Everyday accidents such as closing a finger in a door or drawer commonly cause blood under the nail, called a subungual hematoma. About 50% of nail bed injuries come with an associated distal phalanx fracture. If the trauma injures the matrix, a ridge or split can develop. https://www.harvardhealth.org/a_to_z/nail-trauma-a-to-z and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10498896/

16. **Years: the extreme case.** Shridhar Chillal had not cut the fingernails on his left hand since 1952, and they reached a cumulative 909.6 cm (358.1 in) when measured on 17 November 2014. His coiled thumbnail was 197.8 cm (6 ft 6 in). The weight left his hand in a permanently fixed position, unable to separate his fingers. He said the nails were so fragile he woke every half hour to move his hand to the other side of the bed. https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/746374-longest-fingernail-ever, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-07-13/worlds-longest-fingernails-cut-after-66-years/9987432 and https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2015/9/record-holder-profile-video-shridhar-chillal-and-the-longest-fingernails-ever-398817

**BODY SIGNALS AND MYTHS**

17. **Nails keep a diary of illness.** At about 1 mm of growth per week, a groove halfway up the nail marks an event two to three months back. Beau's lines are transverse depressions caused by a temporary arrest of matrix proliferation, most often linked to severe systemic illness, metabolic stress or cytotoxic drugs. A case report found lines appearing at the proximal nail fold about four to five weeks after each chemotherapy dose. Uncut nails would show this record for months. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-fast-do-fingernails-grow-the-speed-of-you-measured/ and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12889403/

18. **Myth: "Nails keep growing after death."** After death, dehydration makes the skin around the nails dry up and retract, so nails look longer. This is why the myth feels true. Morticians sometimes inject tissue filler into the fingertips to reduce the effect. https://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/do-hair-and-nails-keep-growing-after-we-die-heres-what-science-says/KB755OJE2BD4NN7BOM4RS2PFMM/ and https://theconversation.com/why-do-some-peoples-hair-and-nails-grow-quicker-than-mine-241556

**Extra surprises**
- Nail growth decreases by about 0.5 percent per year starting around age 25. https://biologyinsights.com/how-fast-do-fingernails-grow-the-science-of-nail-growth/
- Nails are about 10–30 percent water (the sources disagree on the exact figure, from about 12% to about 18%), and that water influences flexibility and tensile strength. https://www.bioscience.com.pk/en/subject/biology/why-nails-grow-and-do-not-feel-pain-the-science-behind-nail-formation-and-pain-free-trimming
- A counterintuitive finding: a science-fair project (not peer-reviewed) found nail-biters had the fewest germs under their nails, probably because they swallow them. https://depthtml.musc.edu/catalyst/2007/co2-23nailbiters.html

**Cautions for scripting**
- The "inside the body" effects are mostly local (nail bed, matrix, microbes) rather than organ-level. Don't claim uncut nails damage internal organs. The documented harms are infection risk, trauma and, in extreme cases, hand deformity.
- Several figures come from older or small studies, such as the 18-worker study and the MRSA-in-half-of-samples claim from secondary news reports. Present them as "one study found," not as settled numbers.
