## Facts, in timeline order

**Hours 0–24: the first slide**
1. **17–19 hours awake is about a 0.05% blood alcohol level.** Staying awake 17–19 hours impairs performance more than a 0.05% blood alcohol level, and slows reaction time by about 50%. This is the same as staying up until midnight after a 5 AM wake-up. (Source study: Williamson & Feyer, *Occup Environ Med*, 2000.) https://sleep.hms.harvard.edu/education-training/public-education/sleep-and-health-education-program/sleep-health-education-89
2. **24 hours awake is about 0.10% BAC.** One sleepless night can impair performance as much as a 0.10% blood-alcohol level, beyond the US legal driving limit. Same Harvard source as above. Note that some sites round this to 0.08%, so I'd cite 0.10% from Harvard.
3. **The part of the brain that judges your impairment fails first.** The prefrontal cortex, which handles logical reasoning, seems especially vulnerable. Experts think this is why people struggle to recognise their own impairment. https://sleep.hms.harvard.edu/education-training/public-education/sleep-and-health-education-program/sleep-health-education-89
4. **Everyday hook: six-hour nights.** Two weeks of 6-hour nights is claimed to produce impairment equal to 24–48 hours of total deprivation, while people rate themselves as only mildly impaired. This comes from a secondary site, so check the original experiment (Van Dongen et al., *Sleep*, 2003) before using it. https://thesleepconsultant.com/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep/
5. **Stress hormones rise.** Cortisol and adrenaline climb, which can make you feel wired even as your brain struggles. This is why you get a "second wind". https://scienceinsights.org/how-long-can-a-person-stay-up-limits-and-effects/

**Hours 24–48: the brain starts to break**
6. **The brain's wiring for emotion weakens.** The circuitry linking the prefrontal cortex to the amygdala weakens measurably after 24 hours of sleep loss. The source is a secondary site, so confirm against the original imaging work. https://asmr.education/faq/sleep-deprivation/psychological-consequences-24-hour-sleep-deprivation
7. **Surprising twist: an antidepressant effect around hour 32.** Acute sleep deprivation has an antidepressant effect in some patients, peaking around 32 hours. The same bioRxiv study scanned 41 young adults after 32 hours awake and found widespread changes in cortical microstructure. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.06.22.449439.full.pdf
8. **A single night raises Alzheimer's-linked proteins.** One night of sleep deprivation increased amyloid-β accumulation in the hippocampus of healthy subjects. Another human study found slower glymphatic tracer flow 24–48 hours after sleep deprivation. https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(24)01328-7
9. **The brain's "waste-drain" runs mainly in sleep.** The glymphatic system is a brain-wide network where CSF and interstitial fluid exchange, clearing waste into the CSF and then the blood. During sleep the interstitial space grows about 60%, lowering resistance to fluid flow. That 60% figure comes from the mouse work the review cites. https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/physiol.00019.2024
10. **Human trial, with a nuance.** In a randomized crossover trial of 39 participants, glymphatic clearance during normal sleep raised morning plasma Alzheimer's biomarkers compared with sleep deprivation. During sleep deprivation, release of Aβ and tau was the dominant factor. The result is not simply "you accumulate junk". Say "linked to" rather than "proves". https://doaj.org/article/35470739f3d649d7a1e7c9d796456b45
11. **Microsleeps.** After about 48 hours, involuntary sleep episodes lasting a few seconds to around 15 occur, during which the brain effectively shuts off. The viewer will recognise this as nodding off at the wheel. Another source puts the onset at roughly 36–48 hours. https://thesleepconsultant.com/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep/

**Hours 48–72: day three**
12. **Hallucinations build in stages.** They typically start as misperceptions of real objects around 48 hours and progress to full hallucinations by about 72 hours. By 72 hours, research documents illusions, paranoid thinking, tremor, chills, and raised heart rate and blood pressure. These effects generally resolve with adequate rest. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep-deprivation-hour-by-hour/
13. **Immune system suppression.** At 48–72 hours the immune system is measurably suppressed and stress hormones are significantly elevated. A review notes that prolonged sleep deprivation disrupts cortisol rhythms and may raise susceptibility to infection. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09727531241275347
14. **Counterintuitive: muscles hold up.** A review cited in a 72-hour case report found sleep deprivation up to 72 hours does not affect muscle strength or reaction time, but does shorten time to exhaustion. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7739356/

**Metabolism, in days of lost sleep**
15. **Insulin resistance builds fast.** Across human studies, one night of total sleep deprivation raised insulin resistance by 18%, and three nights of disrupted slow-wave sleep raised it by 25%. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3418485/
16. **Evening cortisol flattens the daily curve.** Sleep loss raises evening cortisol and lowers daytime testosterone. Adding circadian misalignment from simulated night-shift work roughly doubles the insulin resistance compared with sleep loss alone. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07494084

**Extremes, myths and records**
17. **The record: Randy Gardner, 264 hours (11 days), in 1964.** He became moody, had trouble concentrating, lost short-term memory, hallucinated and became consumed by paranoia. He recovered cognitively over a few nights of sleep. Guinness discontinued the category in 1989, citing health concerns. https://www.discovermagazine.com/what-happens-when-we-go-without-sleep-43041
18. **Myth: "Never sleeping" can't kill a healthy person, but a disease can.** There is no confirmed case of a healthy person dying solely from staying awake. Fatal familial insomnia starts with mild insomnia in middle age and progresses to total sleep loss, then deterioration of brain regions controlling breathing and temperature, ending in coma and death. It is a prion disease, not ordinary sleeplessness. In healthy people, sleep pressure forces them to lose consciousness before any lethal point. https://www.risescience.com/blog/how-long-can-you-go-without-sleep

## Caveats for scripting
- **"Never sleeping" for weeks or months is not documented in healthy people.** The weeks-to-months part of your timeline can only be told through chronic partial sleep loss or fatal familial insomnia, which progresses over 12–18 months through worsening insomnia, panic attacks, hallucinations, rapid weight loss and dementia. https://mattressmiracle.ca/blogs/mattress-miracle-blog/russian-sleep-experiment
- **Source quality is uneven.** The Harvard, Cell Reports, Physiology and PMC links are strong. Several timeline items (microsleeps, immune suppression, hallucination timing) come from clinic or blog pages. Before publishing, I'd verify them against NIH/NHLBI, Mayo Clinic or the original studies.
- **Some sources disagree.** For example, one 72-hour case report concluded no psycho-physiological stress was recorded, a single-participant result that contrasts with the broader literature.
