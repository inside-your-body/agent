## Timeline: seconds to months

**Seconds to minutes**

1. **A gamma-wave surge in the first ~30 seconds.** In rats, a transient surge of synchronous gamma oscillations occurred within the first 30 s after cardiac arrest and preceded isoelectric EEG. This is surprising because the brain is usually assumed to simply shut down. (PNAS 2013) https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1308285110
2. **The same surge was seen in 2 of 4 dying humans.** Two of four comatose dying patients had a surge in brain activity resembling consciousness after ventilators were removed and their hearts had stopped. The activity was in the temporal-parietal-occipital junction, the "hot zone" of consciousness correlates. Gamma waves don't necessarily prove that someone is conscious. (PNAS 2023, via Michigan Medicine and Live Science) https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/evidence-conscious-activity-dying-brain
3. **A human case report found the same pattern.** In an 87-year-old patient, after cardiac arrest, delta, beta, alpha and gamma power decreased but relative gamma power was higher than at baseline. (PMC) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8902637/
4. **Skin goes pale within minutes.** Postmortem skin becomes pale, ashen, and loses elasticity within a few minutes after death. Muscles relax immediately after death, then progressively stiffen. (StatPearls/NIH) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK539741/
5. **Cells begin self-digesting at about 4 minutes.** Forensic scientist Dr. Arpad Vass says decomposition begins around four minutes after a person dies. The mechanism: excess carbon dioxide makes the cells acidic and their membranes rupture, releasing enzymes that eat the cells from the inside out. This is autolysis. (Aftermath is a cleanup company, so treat the "4 minutes" figure as a single expert's estimate.) https://aftermath.com/blog/stages-human-decomposition

**Hours**

6. **Blood pooling starts within 30–60 minutes.** Livor mortis begins within 30 min to an hour post-death and reaches maximum visibility within 8-12 h. It's caused by gravity. It is also spared where pressure stops blood entering the skin (floor, tight clothing). (Medscape/StatPearls) https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/1680032-overview
7. **Why the body stiffens: ATP runs out.** Rigor mortis is primarily due to ATP depletion, which leads to permanent actin-myosin cross-bridges. Muscles need energy to release, not just to contract. (Medscape) https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/1680032-overview
8. **Rigor timeline: 1–2 h, peak at 12 h, gone by 24–48 h.** It begins within 1 to 2 hours, is fully established by 12 hours, and resolves over the next 24 to 48 hours. It follows Nysten's law, affecting smaller muscles (eyelids, jaw) before larger ones. (StatPearls/NIH) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK539741/
9. **Rigor can come back.** Rigor can re-establish after mechanical loosening up to 20 h postmortem. Once rigor is fixed and then broken, it normally doesn't return. (Medscape; PathologyOutlines) https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/1680032-overview
10. **Cooling takes about 18–20 hours to reach room temperature.** Algor mortis is cooling to ambient temperature over about 18–20 hours. This is the basis of the forensic "time of death" estimate. (ScienceABC, a secondary source) https://www.scienceabc.com/humans/post-mortemstages-of-death-different-stages-the-body-goes-through-after-death
11. **Some organs stay usable for hours.** Hearts and lungs have only about 4–6 hours. Corneas must be removed within twelve hours of death, and tissues can be donated up to 24 hours after the heart has stopped. (Donate Life CT, an organ procurement organisation) https://www.donatelifect.org/understanding-organ-donation

**Days**

12. **Genes switch ON after death (the "thanatotranscriptome").** In mice and zebrafish, 1,063 genes changed significantly after death, tracked up to 48 or 96 h. They include stress, immunity, inflammation and cancer-related genes. Most upregulation occurred between 0.5 and 24 h, then stopped. This is animal data, not human. (Pozhitkov et al., bioRxiv preprint, later published) https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/058305v2
13. **Microbes take over.** Death causes circulation, immune system, and homeostatic regulation to all cease, allowing previously restricted microbes to grow. Gut anaerobes such as Clostridium dominate. Internal organs like the liver can remain sterile for up to five days. (Sci Reports 2016; Frontiers in Microbiology 2016) https://www.nature.com/articles/srep29598
14. **Bloat starts at about 3–5 days.** Anaerobic gut bacteria multiply and release methane and hydrogen sulfide, and the body can bloat to almost twice its size. Blood-containing foam leaks from the mouth and nose. In hot, humid conditions, bloat begins within 1–3 days. (Forensic and cleanup sources, not peer-reviewed) https://spauldingdecon.com/blog/what-are-the-stages-of-decomposition

**Weeks to months**

15. **Colour changes and liquefaction.** At 8–10 days the body turns from green to red as blood decomposes and abdominal organs fill with gas. Around days 5–10, tissues liquefy into a thick, semisolid black mass. (Forensic medicine lecture syllabus, BHU) https://www.bhu.ac.in/Content/Syllabus/Syllabus_300620200513125809.pdf
16. **Body fat can turn to "grave wax" (adipocere).** It's formed by anaerobic bacterial hydrolysis of fat. It appears at about three weeks at the earliest and commonly takes about three months to develop. Once formed, it can persist for years, even centuries. In 2008, some German cemeteries found bodies expected to skeletonize after 15–25 years were preserved as "soap mummies". (Forensic sources; Wikipedia for the chemistry) https://simplyforensic.com/adipocere-formation-a-fascinating-process-in-forensic-science/
17. **Mummification is the opposite path.** In hot, dry, ventilated conditions, tissue desiccates faster than bacteria can proliferate. A body in a hot, dry attic may take two to three months for partial mummification. (ForensicSpot) https://forensicspot.com/topics/forensic-medicine/decomposition-putrefaction-adipocere-mummification
18. **Skeletonisation can be fast or very slow.** In summer conditions, a human body can skeletonize in nine days. A buried body can take much longer, from months to years. In soil, samples buried directly in the earth fully skeletonized within just 9 months. Sealed tombs retained about 47–48% of initial weight even after 18–24 months. (Wikipedia; a funeral-industry site) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpse_decomposition

## Myths to bust

- **"Hair and nails keep growing after death."** This is false. The skin may retract around them, making the hair and nails jut out more prominently. Dehydration shrinks the skin and soft tissues. (Snopes; UAMS Health) https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/coffin-nails/
- **"The brain just switches off instantly."** The gamma surge data in items 1–3 complicates this, but it is early and based on very few patients.
- **"Decomposition starts days later."** Self-digestion begins within minutes, though it's invisible from the outside.
- **"Rigor mortis is permanent."** It fades over 24–48 hours, as secondary relaxation occurs with the onset of putrefaction.

## Source quality and gaps

- Strongest sources: StatPearls/NIH, PNAS, Scientific Reports, Frontiers and the PMC reviews.
- Weaker sources: the decomposition-stage timelines come mostly from forensic-cleanup and educational sites, not WHO, CDC, NHS or Mayo Clinic. My searches didn't return pages from those organisations, so verify the numbers against a forensic pathology textbook before putting them on screen.
- Organ viability times differ between sources (for example, heart "4 hours" versus "4–6 hours"). Use ranges.
