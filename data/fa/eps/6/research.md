## Timeline: a tablet's journey

**0–60 seconds: the swallow (esophagus)**
1. **About 1 in 5 swallows gets held up.** In an X-ray study of 121 volunteers, pills were held up in the food pipe for more than 90 seconds in 22% of swallows, including those taken lying down or with only a sip of water. (Secondary summary of the study; verify against the original before use.) https://pillo.care/blog/pill-stuck-in-throat
2. **Posture and water matter.** A pill that sticks to the esophageal wall can dissolve there, causing irritation or even ulceration (pill esophagitis). The advice is to stay upright for at least 10 minutes, or 30 for higher-risk pills. https://pillo.care/blog/how-long-does-a-pill-take-to-dissolve
3. **High-risk pills.** Doxycycline, bisphosphonates, NSAIDs, iron and potassium can irritate the esophagus and cause pain or a lodged feeling. https://www.planetdrugsdirect.com/articles/why-does-it-feel-like-my-pill-is-stuck-in-my-throat

**Minutes: the stomach**
4. **Water leaves the stomach fast.** In young healthy adults, 240 mL of still water empties from the stomach within 15–45 minutes, fasted or fed. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10674960/
5. **Food changes the timing a lot.** Median gastric emptying time of a tablet was 37 minutes fasted and 110 minutes fed. https://www.academia.edu/figures/16215018/table-3-did-not-empty-from-the-stomach-transit-and
6. **Big tablets can be held back for hours.** Large particles, including tablets and capsules, can be delayed from emptying for 3–6 hours by food in the stomach. (University lecture notes, not a primary source.) https://uomus.edu.iq/img/lectures21/MUCLecture_2024_51414736.pdf
7. **Stomach acid is strong and variable.** Gastric pH stays below 2 about 68% of the time, while fed-state values range from 2.7 to 6.4. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3025274
8. **A tablet's first minutes can be measured.** In one gamma-scintigraphy study, 75% of subjects on a fast-dissolving paracetamol formulation showed disintegration within 5 minutes, versus none on the standard tablet. (This is a patent example, so it is a company-run result.) https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8604084

**30 minutes to 3 hours: the small intestine, where absorption happens**
9. **Paracetamol blood levels peak quickly.** Peak plasma concentration is at 0.5 to 2 hours, peak effect at 1 to 3 hours, and duration of action 3 to 4 hours. Pain relief starts 15–30 minutes after oral dosing. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5164/smpc and https://www.jpsmjournal.com/article/S0885-3924(13)00449-1/fulltext
10. **Fed or fasted can change the peak time.** Peak plasma concentration is about 20 minutes fasting, but 1–2 hours if gastric emptying is delayed. https://www.jpsmjournal.com/article/S0885-3924(13)00449-1/fulltext
11. **The small intestine is a fixed-length conveyor.** Tablet small-intestinal transit was median 204 minutes fasted and 210 minutes fed. The stomach is the variable part, and the intestine is steady at about 3 hours. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11095-008-9749-2
12. **Eating can speed the tablet up.** When food arrived while a tablet was in the upper small intestine, median transit time dropped to about 100 minutes. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11095-008-9749-2

**The surprising moment: the gut wall and liver act before the drug reaches your blood**
13. **The portal vein sends drugs to the liver first.** Portal veins carry blood directly from the gut to the liver, which allows first-pass metabolism. This is why some drugs need much higher oral doses. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7508170/
14. **The gut wall also metabolizes drugs.** The first-pass effect can occur in the gut, liver and lung. The liver is the main site, but the gut wall plays an important role for some drugs. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2979678/
15. **Grapefruit juice acts inside the intestinal wall.** Intestinal CYP3A4 falls by 47% within 4 hours of drinking grapefruit juice, and the effect can last up to 24 hours. As little as 250 mL can change the metabolism of some drugs. Because the effect lasts so long, separating the juice and the drug does not prevent the interaction. https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/S0025-6196(11)64645-4/fulltext and https://www.cmaj.ca/content/167/3/279
16. **Why grapefruit is so far-reaching.** CYP3A4 is involved in the metabolism of around 50% of drugs. (Review article.) https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379423049_Review_of_grapefruit_juice-drugs_interactions_mediated_by_intestinal_CYP3A4_inhibition

**Hours to a day: clearance by liver and kidney**
17. **Paracetamol is cleared mostly as inactive conjugates.** Mainly as glucuronide (50–60% of urinary metabolites) and sulfate (25–30%). About 85% of a dose is excreted within 24 hours. The half-life is about 1–3 hours. A minor toxic intermediate can build up in overdose (>150 mg/kg or 10 g). https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/76/NCT06855576/Prot_000.pdf
18. **Time in the large intestine is long.** Large-intestine transit is approximately 35 hours. (From a patent, so treat it as approximate.) https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5536507

**Days to months: building up and washing out**
19. **The "5 half-lives" rule.** Steady-state concentration is reached in about 5 half-lives, with notable exceptions. After 1 half-life you are at 50% of steady state, after 3 at 87.5%, and after 5 at about 97%. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553132/ and https://www.certara.com/knowledge-base/understanding-steady-state-pharmacokinetics/
20. **The dose doesn't change the timing.** Time to steady state depends only on half-life, not dose. A bigger dose raises the level but doesn't get you there faster. A 24-hour half-life means about 4–5 days to steady state, and a 7-day half-life means 4–5 weeks. This is why antidepressants and similar drugs take days to weeks. https://www.dexocalc.com/health-calculators/drug-half-life-calculator/
21. **Kidney problems slow clearance.** Drugs cleared unchanged by the kidneys (gabapentin, metformin, lithium, digoxin) can have half-lives two to five times longer when kidney function is reduced. (Pharmacist-built calculator; verify against a primary source.) https://www.dexocalc.com/health-calculators/drug-half-life-calculator/

## Myths and everyday situations
- **Myth: a pill in your stool means it didn't work.** Controlled-release capsule shells can appear undigested in stool. In a study of nevirapine extended-release tablets, no loss of efficacy was reported despite tablet remnants in stool. One report notes the "ghost" is only about 9% of the original pellet mass. https://www.proquest.com/scholarly-journals/undigested-pills-stool-mimicking-parasitic/docview/1867575197/se-2 and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264432445
- **Don't crush extended-release pills.** Crushing destroys the time-release mechanism and can spike blood levels. (Pharmacy education site; check the drug label.) https://rx.com/education/undigested-pills-in-stool-metformin-ghost-tablet
- **Enteric-coated pills wait for the intestine.** The coating resists acid and dissolves only at the higher pH of the small intestine, delaying release by 1–2 hours or more. (Manufacturer blog; verify before use.) https://www.jinlupacking.com/blogs/how-long-for-pills-to-dissolve/
- **Dry-swallowing a fast-dissolving tablet can change timing.** For alprazolam, Tmax was 1.75 h for the conventional tablet, and 2 h and 2.25 h for the dissolving tablet with and without water. Water negated the dissolving tablet's bioavailability advantage by preventing partial absorption through the mouth lining. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41120-025-00138-y
- **Pill stuck in your throat?** Sit upright, drink water, and see a doctor if it persists. If discomfort lasts more than 2 hours, seek medical attention. (Clinical Q&A site.) https://www.droracle.ai/articles/288566/what-to-do-if-a-pill-gets-stuck-in
