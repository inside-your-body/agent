I'll run several searches in parallel to cover the mechanism, the epidemiology, triggers, and the sleep-timeline context.Here are 16 facts on hypnic jerks (sleep starts), ordered roughly as a timeline. Not every source is a top-tier institution, so I've flagged the best-sourced ones. I did not find pages from WHO, CDC, NHS or Mayo Clinic in these searches, so I haven't cited them.

**Minutes before and at sleep onset (the mechanism)**

1. **Hypnic jerks affect most people.** 60–70% of individuals experience hypnic jerks, usually just as they are about to fall asleep. People who get them don't usually have them every time they sleep. https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324666

2. **The true share may be higher.** A 2016 study in Sleep Medicine puts the figure at 60–70%, and the true share is probably higher, since most jolts are too small to remember. https://www.forbes.com/sites/scotttravers/2026/07/31/why-your-body-jerks-right-before-you-fall-asleep---a-biologist-explains-the-hypnic-jerk/

3. **It happens in the first minutes of sleep.** Jerks typically occur at the boundary between wakefulness and stage N1 non-REM sleep, when brain waves begin to slow but deeper sleep hasn't kicked in. N1 usually lasts one to seven minutes. https://www.sleepfoundation.org/stages-of-sleep

4. **Brain waves shift as you drift off.** Sleep onset slows brain frequency to alpha (8–12 Hz) drowsiness, and finally to theta (4–10 Hz) in Stage 1 NREM sleep. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience_of_sleep

5. **The body is winding down at the same time.** In N1, there is a decrease in heart rate, muscle tension and body temperature, although the body has not completely relaxed yet. https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/physiology/stages-of-sleep

6. **A chemical handover happens in the brain.** The hypothalamic ventrolateral preoptic nucleus produces the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA, which inhibits the arousal system at sleep onset. Acetylcholine, norepinephrine, serotonin and histamine, all wakefulness-promoting, decrease with sleep onset. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_onset

7. **The jerk is thought to start in the brainstem, not the muscles.** Jerks presumably arise from sudden descending volleys that originate in the brainstem reticular formation and are activated by the instability of the system during the wake-to-sleep transition. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10590197/

8. **The motor "handover" leading theory.** A stray burst of activity in the motor pathways slips through before inhibition is fully in place, and reaches the muscles as a single hard contraction. https://www.forbes.com/sites/scotttravers/2026/07/31/why-your-body-jerks-right-before-you-fall-asleep---a-biologist-explains-the-hypnic-jerk/

9. **It involves almost the whole body.** Hypnic jerks are abrupt myoclonic muscle contractions involving nearly all body muscles that occur mainly on falling asleep. One recording showed abrupt involvement of neck, trunk and limb muscles. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10590197/

10. **The jerk has an EEG signature.** EEG research suggests a specific brain activity pattern during a hypnic jerk, known as vertex sharp waves. https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324666

**Hours before bed (triggers)**

11. **The American Academy of Sleep Medicine lists the usual suspects.** They include high caffeine consumption, intense workouts, sleep deprivation, and stress. https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/health/causes-of-hypnic-jerk-during-sleep

12. **Caffeine does delay sleep, but its link to jerks is unproven.** A 2023 meta-analysis found caffeine lengthens time to fall asleep by roughly nine minutes. No controlled trial has shown that any of the usual triggers causes more hypnic jerks. https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/hypnic-jerks-sleep-starts

13. **Being more tired may make them worse.** When you are overtired, your brain tends to plunge into sleep faster and more abruptly, which seems to make sleep starts more likely. Some medications, including stimulants, some antidepressants and antipsychotics, are also known to induce them. https://benignfasciculationsyndrome.org/blog/hypnic-jerks

**Days to months (the anxiety loop)**

14. **A fear loop can build over days and weeks.** Jerks cause waking, waking causes fear, fear raises arousal, and higher arousal produces more jerks. Intensified jerks can interfere with sleep onset and cause insomnia. https://www.tracythesleepcoach.co.uk/sleep-learning/hypnic-jerk/

15. **Chronic sleep loss from intense jerks may feed back on mood.** Intensified jerks can cause chronic sleep deprivation, potentially resulting in depression through neurochemical changes in the brain. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10590197/

16. **Jerks are a "developmental feature" present from early life.** Children can experience them from birth, and one 2003 study describes myoclonus as a developmental feature of the human nervous system, present from the early stages of fetal development. https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324666

**Myths to bust**

- **"You were falling out of a tree."** This is a popular story, but the "falling out of a tree" evolutionary theory has no supporting evidence. It is a hypothesis, not established fact. https://www.ageproof.co.uk/learn/sleep-circadian/hypnic-jerks/
- **"It's a seizure."** Jerks arise solely at sleep onset, exhibit a normal EEG, and do not involve tongue bites, urinary incontinence, or postictal confusion. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11964172/
- **"It's a sign of ALS."** Hypnic jerks are not a sign of ALS, which is defined by progressive weakness. This comes from a niche site, so check it against a clinical source before using it. https://benignfasciculationsyndrome.org/blog/hypnic-jerks
- **"Calcium or magnesium deficiency causes them."** I found no solid evidence for this: there are no trials of magnesium for hypnic jerks specifically.

**Caveats for the script**

- The cause is still unknown. There is no single definitive reason uncovered so far. Present the brainstem and motor-inhibition account as the leading theory, not settled fact.
- Many popular numbers (the 60–70% figure, the trigger lists) come from review articles and clinical observation. They do not come from controlled trials.
- Some of the best primary sources are the 2014 Sleep Medicine paper (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24815789/) and the 2015 Sleep Medicine Clinics review (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1556407X15000454). I could only see their abstracts or citations.
